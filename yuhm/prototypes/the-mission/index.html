THE MISSION

yuhm is a free food network for Austin, Texas. Open it and you see free food near you: community fridges, pantries, shared harvests. No account needed. Neighbors who want to help use the same app to move food to where it is needed: rescuing day-old bread, sharing garden extras, cooking together, and returning scraps to the soil.

This page is a clickable demo and a build plan for two ideas. Neither is built yet. THE MISSION is for neighbors who want to help: say yes once, and yuhm offers you small, clear tasks nearby, like checking that a fridge is stocked or driving bread across town, at a weekly pace you choose. Gatherings is for cooking together: each person says what they want to make, gets an itemized grocery list, and finds their items waiting in a numbered bin. The task flow borrows what works from DoorDash and Duolingo and leaves their pressure tactics behind. Finding food never becomes a game.

Ground rules

Five lines the design does not cross

  • Finding food is never a game.The finder, the map, and FOOD IS HERE alerts stay exactly as they are. No seeds, no prompts, no account wall. A test will assert it.
  • A separate yes.An account does not enroll anyone. THE MISSION is its own opt-in, and pause and leave are one tap each.
  • Mechanics, not manipulation.PRODUCT.md already lists delivery-app urgency as an anti-reference. We take accept, steps, done, and a weekly rhythm. We leave countdown panic, guilt messages, and acceptance-rate penalties.
  • An honest board.Only real missions. Samples are labeled. A quiet neighborhood says it is quiet instead of inventing activity.
  • Private by default.Neighborhood-level until acceptance. No public ranking of people. Shared goals belong to the circle.

Bites solve the cold start. A mission board with no missions is dead on day one. Seeds and levels

Seeds are recognition. They have no cash value, are never required for anything, and are never taken away. They are stored as recognition_points in the ledger that already exists. Reliability is a separate, private signal the dispatcher uses; nobody ever sees it as a score.

Levels: Seed 0 · Sprout 50 · Vine 150 · Bloom 400 · Harvest 900 · Perennial 2,000. Levels are thanks. Safety gates stay on trust_tier, which coordinators set.

Weekly rhythm, with rest built in

A week counts when at least one mission of any size is completed (Monday to Sunday, Austin time). The 1, 2, or 3 goal fills the ring; it never breaks the rhythm. One rest week is banked for every four active weeks (two at most) and spent automatically. Pausing freezes the rhythm. If it does end, the app says “new rhythm, week 1” and keeps the longest one on the profile. No message ever mentions losing it.

Stamps and the circle goal

Stamps mark firsts and milestones: First bite, First rescue, Full circle (a compost return after a rescue), Bilingual (ten translations), Four-week rhythm. The circle goal is one shared weekly target per neighborhood circle, such as pounds moved. It takes the place a leaderboard would. The existing opt-in drop-off board from migration 040 stays as it is.

Keeping it fair

Bites cap at five a day. An hours change needs two independent confirmations and each location has a 14-day cooldown. Seeds for runs land when the receiving side confirms or a coordinator reviews. The ledger is append-only with idempotency keys, so retries never double-pay.

Functions, in the repo’s naming

For members: enroll_food_mission, update_food_mission_enrollment, leave_food_mission, list_food_missions, accept_food_mission, pass_food_mission, get_food_mission_private, record_food_mission_checkpoint, release_food_mission, get_food_mission_progress, set_food_notification_prefs. For workers only (is_food_worker_caller()): dispatch_food_missions, expire_food_mission_offers, roll_food_mission_week, generate_food_bites.

  • No double-booking. accept_food_mission locks the mission row (select … for update), checks the slot count and the offer’s expiry, then writes the assignment and bumps the count in one transaction.
  • Completion is one transaction: assignment, ledger row, progress cache, stamps, outbox row. Either all of it lands or none of it does.
  • Row-level security. People read only their own enrollment, offers, assignments, checkpoints, progress, prefs, and log. Open missions expose public_summary only. Every write goes through a security-definer function. Coordinators (role = 'admin') manage templates and missions.
  • Migration. The next free number is 049 today, but koord/ and the client sites share the folder, so take the number at build time. Validate the whole chain in the podman Supabase image first. Migrate first, deploy second.
  • No dependency on the undeployed services. The 032 to 036 tables are applied but only the Dockerized coordination API uses them. Phase 1 reaches them through new functions from the browser, the same way the first-generation workflows do.

WhatsApp helper

A one-to-one helper now, group news through people

The group-chat idea has a hard limit. On the official Cloud API, groups cap at 8 members including the business number, need an Official Business Account (the verified badge), and cannot show buttons. Libraries that pose as a phone can sit in any group, but they break WhatsApp’s terms and get numbers banned, which is not a risk to take with the network’s main line. So the helper lives in one-to-one threads, and each circle gets a human-run announcement group where a coordinator posts the weekly update the dashboard writes for them. A run crew of seven or fewer could get an official group later, if HAND earns the badge.

What the helper can do

Each tool is one of the functions above, run under that person’s mandate: list my missions and offers, accept, pass, release, record a checkpoint (“picked up”), report my rhythm and seeds, pause and resume, change my rhythm goal, stop WhatsApp, find food near a neighborhood from the public directory, and hand off to a coordinator.

What it must never do

  • Act for anyone but the number it is talking to
  • Share another person’s name, number, or address
  • Reveal an address before acceptance
  • Pretend to be a person. It says it is automated and offers a human at any time.
  • Handle safety, illness, allergy, conflict, or urgent need alone. It gives the food map link first, then flags a coordinator.
  • Name a specific AI model in public copy (existing HAND rule)
  • Where it runs. A Netlify function verifies Meta’s signature, stores the message, answers 200, and triggers a background function that runs the helper and replies. Same repo, no new servers. When the coordination stack moves to OVH (plan 003, phase 3) the helper can move next to HandAI.
  • Long pole: Meta business verification. It wants legal-entity documents. The Texas Certificate of Formation and the EIN should be enough, but it can take days to weeks, so start it during phase 1. Also needed: a number that has never been on WhatsApp, display-name review for “yuhm”, and five utility templates in English and Spanish (offer, reminder, receipt, released, opt-in confirmation).
  • Cost sketch. 100 opted-in neighbors at about six templates a week is roughly 600 × $0.006, under $4 a week. Replies are free. Model usage for conversation is the larger line, held down by keyword shortcuts and short context.
  • Phone numbers are sensitive. Stored encrypted (address_ciphertext), matched by keyed fingerprint, deleted on leave.

Gatherings beta

Cook together, each from your own list

Added 20 Sep 2026. koord, the booking and coordination layer, will sit under every HAND feature in time. The first thing it powers in yuhm is a beta for gatherings and classes, and the first worked case is a food prep gathering: each person says what they want to prepare, the app writes their itemized list, and on the day their items wait in a numbered bin. The five screens are the last group in the demo above.

Choices that carry the weight

  • The source is per line, not per person. Bring my own rice, network cilantro, buy the chicken.
  • Recipe kits on a small item catalog, written in units a store sells. That is what lets twelve lists add up into one.
  • Pantry items are pooled. Three people need cumin and the shopper buys one jar. Everything else: you pay for what was bought for you.
  • The cutoff is the commitment. Cancel freely before it. After it your buy lines are owed, and you can name someone to collect your bin.
  • Covered spots stay private. Only the organizer and the money holder see who asked.
  • Food safety is in the model: a temperature class per item, a cooler step, a short shop window, and an allergen notice computed from the locked orders.

What is already there

  • Migration 033 has the gathering spine: food_venues, food_events, invites, RSVPs with a waitlist, assignments, and a potluck lane gate. Only the undeployed coordination API uses it today.
  • “Source it from our app” is live first-generation work: inventory lots, harvest runs, rescues, drop-offs.
  • koord brings the time poll, RSVP rules, reminders, deadline notices, and the delivery policy (quiet hours, caps, consent). Proposed: it runs as a library inside yuhm, with yuhm’s tables as the store.
  • A gathering creates missions: shop run, pickup, sort, setup, cleanup, host, teach. Attending never enrolls anyone.
Money, settled by koH on 20 SepDirect settle, done by hand. The first implementation is a checklist the money holder ticks: paid, covered, shop anyway, or drop, then a second tick for settled. Every tick records who and when and can be undone. No payment handles stored, no processor, and HAND never holds the pot. Stripe on the organizer’s own account is a later phase and needs a /hand-tax pass first.

Still open for koH

  • Where koord runs for yuhm. Recommendation: as a library inside yuhm.
  • Buffer on estimates. Recommendation: 15 percent, rounded up to the dollar, returned at settle.
  • No refund on bought lines after the cutoff. Recommendation: yes, with name-a-collector and release-to-the-table.
  • Pantry: host kitchen supplies it, or pooled and split. Recommendation: the organizer chooses, default pooled.
  • Sorting at the venue by bin, or bagged per person in the store. Recommendation: at the venue.
  • Free-text dishes in the beta. Recommendation: allowed, but they do not merge.
  • Who can organize. Recommendation: coordinators only during the supervised pilot.
  • Gatherings on the public food map. Recommendation: yes, as a plain listing, when free or with covered spots.

Build phases

Each phase ships something people can use

  1. Sign off on this demo

    Settle the decisions below. Start Meta business verification in parallel, because it is slow and blocks nothing else.

  2. Join flow, opt-in, bites, progress

    In-app only. Migration for enrollments, templates, missions, assignments, checkpoints, progress, stamps. Bites generated from directory gaps. No dispatcher, no cold-start risk.Done when: a new visitor goes landing, first bite, account, opt-in, second bite, and sees their rhythm, in English and Spanish; a test proves the guest food finder shows no mission UI.

  3. Runs, shifts, offers, email

    Offers table, the dispatcher as a scheduled function, adapters for rescues, harvest runs, and drop-offs, a “Make this a mission” button on the coordinator boards, one-tap email links, reminders with calendar files, Sunday digest, check-in.Done when: a coordinator posts a rescue, a matched neighbor accepts from an email, completes it on a phone, and the ledger, rhythm, and receipt all agree.

  4. WhatsApp helper, one-to-one

    Webhook, templates, helper tools, keyword shortcuts, a handoff inbox in the dashboard.Done when: accept, pass, pause, and “talk to a person” all work in English and Spanish from a real phone, and every action appears in food_agent_actions.

  5. Circles, pair-ups, push, smarter matching

    Circle goals from real totals, pair-up missions, web push for “I’m free now”, and the OR-Tools matching worker taking over multi-stop runs.

How we will know it works

Opt-in rate among new accounts. Share of visitors who finish the first bite before sign-up. Sign-up conversion after the first bite against today’s funnel. Missions completed per week. Offer acceptance rate. Time to fill a rescue. Four-week rhythm retention. Pause and leave rates. Unsubscribes and complaints. Share of completions that began from a notification. One counter-metric: guest use of the food finder must not drop.

Decisions for koH

Nine calls to make before phase 1

  • The words: seeds, rhythm, bites, stamps.RecommendationKeep them and add them to the world glossary next to Circle, Spot, Pool, Run.
  • Does the Eat route get missions at all?RecommendationYes, bites only, and offered only after a successful food search. Never before, never as a condition.
  • Weekly rhythm or a daily streak?RecommendationWeekly. Food work runs on a weekly beat, and daily streaks are where the guilt lives.
  • An individual leaderboard?RecommendationNo. Circle goals only. The existing opt-in drop-off board stays.
  • Do seeds ever convert into anything?RecommendationNot in version 1. Anything of value to volunteers raises tax and fairness questions; run it past /hand-tax before promising it.
  • WhatsApp scope.RecommendationOne-to-one helper plus human-run announcement groups. Start Meta verification now.
  • “Take part” on the second landing door for first-time visitors.RecommendationYes, with Sign in kept as a text link right under it.
  • Who creates missions at launch?RecommendationBites generate themselves. Runs and shifts come from coordinators through one “Make this a mission” button on the rescue, harvest, and event boards.
  • The helper’s name and voice.Recommendation“The yuhm helper”, plainspoken, bilingual, always clear that it is automated.

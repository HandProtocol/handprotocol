# Executive Compensation Policy

> Implements the rebuttable-presumption procedure under Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6 for Executive Director compensation. Required disclosure on Form 990 Part VI lines 15a-b.

**Status:** Draft v0.1 · May 2026 · Pre-adoption · Pending counsel review

---

## Section 1. Purpose

This Policy establishes the procedure by which HAND Protocol Foundation (the "**Corporation**") sets, reviews, and documents compensation for its Executive Director, other officers, and key employees, in a manner that:

a. Establishes the IRS's **rebuttable presumption of reasonableness** under Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6, shifting the burden to the IRS to prove unreasonableness rather than to the Corporation to prove reasonableness.

b. Avoids excise tax exposure for "disqualified persons" (potentially the Executive Director) and approving managers under IRC §4958 (intermediate sanctions).

c. Reflects HAND's values: compensation reasonable in light of mission, role complexity, and labor market reality, neither at the floor that risks turnover and burnout nor inflated relative to comparable positions.

## Section 2. Scope

This Policy applies to compensation set for:

a. The Executive Director.

b. Any officer of the Corporation receiving compensation for employment (not for board service, which is uncompensated under the Bylaws).

c. Any other "disqualified person" under IRC §4958 (a person in a position to exercise substantial influence over the Corporation, or a family member or controlled entity of such a person).

d. Any key employee whose total compensation exceeds **$150,000** in a year, or any employee whose compensation is at least 25% above the median for comparable positions in the relevant market.

## Section 3. Compensation Committee

a. **Composition.** The Compensation Committee shall consist of at least two (2) directors, all of whom are independent under Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6(c)(1)(iii): no Financial Interest in the Executive Director's compensation, no subordinate relationship, no compensation set in turn by the person whose compensation is being set, and no family or material business relationship with the person.

b. **Quorum and action.** A majority of committee members shall constitute a quorum. Action is by majority vote of those present.

c. **Recusal.** A committee member with any actual or potential conflict shall disclose the conflict and refuse from the relevant discussion and vote.

d. **Where the Board has fewer than four directors,** the Compensation Committee's role may be performed by the full Board, with the affected person recused. **Recommended path: recruit a fourth director before the first paid Executive Director is hired**, so the Compensation Committee can operate as a true independent body.

## Section 4. Comparability data

Before approving any compensation arrangement subject to this Policy, the Compensation Committee shall obtain and rely upon **appropriate comparability data**.

a. **Sources.** Acceptable sources include:

- Compensation surveys published by Candid (formerly GuideStar) drawing on Form 990 filings.
- Surveys published by BoardSource, the National Council of Nonprofits, or sector-specific associations.
- Salary studies by professional firms (e.g., Nonprofit HR, Charity Navigator's Nonprofit Compensation Report).
- A review of Forms 990 of at least three comparable organizations.
- Compensation studies commissioned by the Corporation.

b. **"Comparable" definition.** Comparability requires similarity in:
- Mission and program type (capacity-building / accompaniment / grassroots focus).
- Annual budget size (within roughly half to double HAND's budget).
- Geographic region (Texas, Southwest, or national if explicitly justified).
- Role complexity (number of staff, programs, locations).

c. **Small-organization safe harbor.** For an organization with **annual gross receipts under $1 million**, the IRS permits reliance on data from **three comparable organizations** as sufficient. HAND will rely on this safe harbor in its early years, and will move to broader comparability surveys (Nonprofit HR or commissioned study) when annual gross receipts reach $1 million.

d. **Documentation.** Comparability data, including source, date, and the comparable positions identified, shall be retained in the Compensation Committee file under the Document Retention Policy (permanent retention as part of board records).

## Section 5. Compensation review procedure

The Compensation Committee shall:

a. **Define the position** under review, including title, scope, responsibilities, expected time commitment, and reporting relationship.

b. **Gather comparability data** per Section 4.

c. **Calculate a recommended compensation range** including base salary, benefits, retirement contributions, paid time off, and any deferred or contingent compensation. **Total compensation**, not just salary, is the unit of analysis.

d. **Consider HAND's compensation ratio commitments.** Per the Code of Ethics, HAND commits to a compensation ratio of no more than **[ratio TBD by board, e.g., 3:1 or 4:1]** between the highest-paid and lowest-paid full-time employees. This ratio is a HAND-specific commitment, not an IRS requirement, but it constrains the upper bound of executive compensation.

e. **Consider non-compensation factors** appropriate to HAND: relational responsibilities to Companions, the public-facing nature of the role, fundraising obligations, and the founder dynamic if applicable.

f. **Deliberate without the affected person present** (the Executive Director or candidate is excused for the deliberation and vote).

g. **Vote** on the recommended compensation arrangement.

h. **Contemporaneously document** the basis for the decision in committee minutes. The minutes shall include:
- The terms of the compensation arrangement (base, benefits, total).
- The comparability data relied upon.
- The committee members present and the vote.
- The reasoning for the chosen level within the comparable range.
- Any dissent and its basis.

The committee shall record the minutes **before the later of:** (i) the next meeting of the committee, or (ii) **sixty (60) days** after the action. The minutes must be approved by the committee within a reasonable time thereafter.

## Section 6. When the procedure is triggered

a. **At hire** of any covered employee.

b. **Annually**, in conjunction with the budget process. The Compensation Committee performs a full review for the Executive Director and a confirmation-of-reasonableness review for other officers and key employees.

c. **At any material change** in compensation terms, including (without limitation):
- Salary increase greater than the percentage approved in the annual budget for general staff.
- Addition of new compensation components (bonus, deferred compensation, equity-like arrangements, though equity is not applicable to a nonprofit).
- Change in benefits structure that increases total compensation by more than 5%.

d. **At role change** material enough to alter the basis for comparability (significant scope change, addition of substantially new responsibilities).

## Section 7. Compensation philosophy

HAND's compensation philosophy:

a. **No poverty-wage roles.** No full-time HAND employee shall be paid less than a living wage for the locale, as published by MIT Living Wage Calculator or equivalent. This is HAND's floor.

b. **No outlier executive pay.** Executive Director compensation shall be in the **40th to 70th percentile** of comparable organizations by default. Above-median pay requires explicit Compensation Committee justification (e.g., unusual fundraising obligations, founder retention).

c. **Compensation ratio.** Highest-paid to lowest-paid full-time compensation ratio shall not exceed a Board-set limit. Default proposal: **3:1** in pre-revenue years, transitioning to **4:1** at organizational maturity.

d. **Transparency.** The Executive Director's total compensation is disclosed on Form 990 Schedule J and is therefore public. HAND will, in addition, disclose total compensation for all named officers and the compensation ratio in the Annual Report.

e. **Equity practice.** Compensation decisions shall consider how the role fits within HAND's broader equity commitments, including race, gender, disability, and lived experience equity. Compensation studies will be reviewed for sample bias and adjusted as warranted.

## Section 8. Excess benefit transactions and intermediate sanctions

a. The Corporation shall not enter into any transaction with a disqualified person on terms more favorable than the Corporation would offer an unrelated party.

b. If, despite the procedures of this Policy, the Corporation discovers that compensation paid was unreasonable (an "excess benefit transaction"), the Corporation shall promptly:

- Recover the excess portion from the disqualified person.
- Document the correction.
- Consult counsel regarding any required disclosures on Form 990 or to the IRS.

c. **Penalties for an uncorrected excess benefit transaction:**
- First-tier tax on the disqualified person: **25%** of the excess benefit.
- Tax on each approving manager who knowingly approved: **10%** of the excess benefit, capped at $20,000.
- Second-tier tax if not corrected within the correction period: **200%** of the excess benefit on the disqualified person.

## Section 9. Founder compensation specifics

If the founder of HAND becomes the first paid Executive Director:

a. The Compensation Committee shall be especially rigorous in establishing comparability, drawing from at least five comparable organizations rather than the three-organization safe harbor.

b. The founder shall recuse from any board discussion of the founder's compensation, including during the Compensation Committee's reporting to the full Board.

c. The Compensation Committee shall consider founder-specific dynamics (the value of founder continuity, the risk of founder dependence) and document the consideration explicitly in the minutes.

d. Founder compensation shall be reviewed by an external compensation consultant at least once every three years, in addition to the annual internal review.

## Section 10. Records and public disclosure

a. All Compensation Committee minutes, comparability data, and supporting documents are retained permanently as part of board records.

b. Executive Director compensation is disclosed on Form 990 Schedule J (public).

c. HAND's compensation philosophy and ratio commitments are published on the website.

d. Salary ranges for all positions are included in job postings, consistent with HAND's transparency commitments and an emerging norm in mission-aligned hiring.

## Open questions

- **Compensation ratio target.** Drafted as 3:1 pre-revenue, 4:1 at maturity. Ben & Jerry's famously used 5:1, then 7:1, before abandoning the limit. Some justice-aligned nonprofits use as low as 2:1. Recommend final ratio be set by full Board, not just Compensation Committee, given its values weight.
- **Living-wage benchmark.** MIT Living Wage Calculator for Travis County, TX. Family-of-four-single-earner figure was $107K/year as of last update. Single-adult figure is closer to $50K. HAND should pick which benchmark applies and apply consistently.
- **External compensation consultant.** Cost (~$5K-15K for a study) hard to justify pre-revenue. Recommended after first full year of operations.
- **Equity-style retention mechanisms.** Not applicable in nonprofit (no equity). Deferred compensation under §457(b) plans is permissible for executives but adds complexity. Probably not needed until org grows substantially.
- **Founder-as-ED transition.** If founder remains as unpaid Executive Director through the filing-raise period and only takes salary post-501(c)(3), the rebuttable-presumption procedure should be run before the first paycheck, not retroactively.

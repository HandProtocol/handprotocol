# Fiscal Sponsorship Policy

> Governs HAND Protocol Foundation's posture toward fiscal sponsorship as a possible interim path during the 501(c)(3) filing period, and the criteria for selecting, working with, and exiting a fiscal sponsor relationship.

**Status:** Draft v0.1 · May 2026 · Pre-adoption

---

## Section 1 — Purpose and decision

The Corporation may operate under a fiscal sponsorship arrangement during the period between organization (Texas SOS filing) and receipt of the IRS Determination Letter recognizing 501(c)(3) status, in order to accept tax-deductible donations from day one.

**Recommended posture:** Use a Model C ("Pre-approved Grant Relationship") fiscal sponsor for an estimated 12–18 month period from organization through receipt of IRS Determination Letter, then exit the sponsor relationship.

**Decision criteria for using fiscal sponsorship at all:**

a. **Use a sponsor** if:
- The filing-raise goal closes before IRS Determination is expected (likely).
- Funder pipeline includes near-term deadlines requiring tax-deductible recipient status.
- The Form 1023 timeline is expected to exceed 6 months.

b. **Skip sponsorship and file directly** if:
- IRS Determination is expected within 4 months (rare; most 1023s take longer).
- Pipeline is primarily long-form grants with deadlines >12 months out.
- Sponsor fees (5–10%) exceed the value of accelerated acceptance.

c. **Defer** the decision if:
- Filing-raise is not yet imminent.
- No clear donor or grant pipeline requires immediate tax-deductible status.

## Section 2 — Sponsorship model

The Corporation will use **Model C — Pre-approved Grant Relationship** per Colvin's *Fiscal Sponsorship: 6 Ways To Do It Right* taxonomy.

Under Model C:

a. The Corporation is a separate legal entity from the sponsor.

b. The sponsor receives tax-deductible donations earmarked for the Corporation and re-grants them under a written grant agreement.

c. The sponsor retains variance power (ability to redirect funds if the Corporation ceases to qualify).

d. The Corporation has operational autonomy and holds its own contracts, employs its own staff (or contractors), and runs its own programs.

The Corporation will **not** use Model A (Comprehensive / Direct Project) sponsorship, because Model A would dissolve HAND into the sponsor and complicate the 1023 application as an independent entity.

## Section 3 — Sponsor selection criteria

A fiscal sponsor must:

a. Be a current 501(c)(3) public charity in good standing with the IRS.

b. Have a documented Model C fiscal sponsorship program with experience hosting at least 25 projects.

c. Have transparent fee structure (no hidden fees, no required minimum dollars).

d. Have insurance covering sponsored projects (general liability at minimum; D&O coverage helpful).

e. Have a written sponsor agreement with clear exit terms.

f. Have a transition-friendly posture: explicit support for sponsored projects spinning out to their own 501(c)(3).

g. Have a fee at or below 8% of donations received.

h. Have a documented mission and Companion-population alignment, or at least no documented conflict with HAND's values.

i. Have responsive operations (response time on inquiries under 5 business days, and ability to issue grant payments within 10 business days of request).

The Executive Director shall conduct a structured comparison and present a recommendation to the Board no later than the meeting at which the sponsorship decision is to be made.

## Section 5 — Sponsor agreement key terms

The Corporation will negotiate for the following terms in any sponsor agreement:

a. **Term.** Initial term of one year, renewable. Exit on **30 days' notice** by either party.

b. **Fee.** Fixed percentage of donations passed through. No surprises.

c. **Variance power.** Sponsor's variance power is preserved (legally required) but bound by good-faith consultation with the Corporation before any redirect.

d. **Donor intent.** Sponsor commits to honoring donor intent in re-grants unless the Corporation ceases to qualify or to operate.

e. **Records.** Sponsor maintains records of donations and grants; Corporation has access to its own records on request.

f. **Exit.** On termination, sponsor disburses remaining funds (after reasonable wind-down fees) to the Corporation, or to a successor 501(c)(3) of the Corporation's choosing, within 60 days.

g. **Insurance.** Sponsor covers Corporation activities under sponsor's general liability and (if applicable) cyber insurance for the sponsorship term.

h. **Branding.** Sponsor permits HAND's branding, public communications, and fundraising under HAND's name.

i. **Sovereign Companions data.** Sponsor acknowledges that Companion-group-owned adapters and data are property of the respective Companion groups, not assets of the sponsor or the Corporation.

j. **Form 990 reporting.** Donations to and through the sponsor are reported on the sponsor's Form 990, not HAND's (until HAND has its own determination).

## Section 6 — Operating under sponsorship

a. **Branding and communications.** HAND operates publicly under its own name. The relationship is disclosed in the footer of donation pages and acknowledgments ("HAND Protocol Foundation is a fiscally sponsored project of [Sponsor]").

b. **Tax-deductible donations.** Donor receipts come from the sponsor. The donor letter states the sponsor's EIN and 501(c)(3) status; HAND is identified as the project.

c. **Banking and finances.** Donations flow into the sponsor's accounts. HAND draws down via grant request from the sponsor on an agreed cadence (typically monthly).

d. **Reporting to sponsor.** HAND provides whatever activity, financial, and impact reports the sponsor requires under the agreement. Frequency is typically quarterly.

e. **Contracts.** HAND can sign its own contracts as a Texas nonprofit corporation, even pre-determination. Some contracts may need to be co-signed by or routed through the sponsor.

f. **Insurance.** Sponsor's coverage is the floor; HAND may carry additional D&O insurance separately if Board service warrants.

g. **Hiring.** Sponsor's employment infrastructure may be used for early hires (sponsor as employer of record) or HAND may run payroll under its own EIN with sponsor's grant funding the payroll. Decided at sponsor agreement signing.

## Section 7 — Exit from sponsorship

a. **Trigger.** Receipt of IRS Determination Letter recognizing 501(c)(3) status, plus completion of Texas Comptroller exemption (AP-204), plus opening of HAND's own bank account ready to receive donations directly.

b. **Notification.** Written notice to sponsor at least 60 days before intended exit date.

c. **Transfer.** Remaining funds in sponsor's hands are transferred to HAND's account, less any reasonable wind-down costs.

d. **Donor communication.** Donors are notified of the transition with a clear message: "HAND Protocol Foundation is now a direct 501(c)(3). Future donations may be made directly to HAND. Donations made through [Sponsor] during the sponsorship period are tax-deductible under [Sponsor]'s status; future donations will be tax-deductible under HAND's status."

e. **Records.** HAND obtains copies of all donor records, financial records, and program records held by the sponsor.

f. **Public acknowledgment.** HAND publicly thanks the sponsor in the year-of-exit Annual Report.

## Section 8 — Funder considerations

a. **Funders that prefer or require direct 501(c)(3) recipients:** MacKenzie Scott / Yield Giving's recent rounds, some federal grants, some Texas community foundations. HAND should accelerate exit from sponsorship when these are in the pipeline.

b. **Funders comfortable with fiscally sponsored projects:** most foundations, individual donors, employer matching programs, DAFs.

c. **Funders requiring direct fiduciary disclosure:** in any case, HAND discloses the sponsor relationship transparently in applications.

## Section 9 — Public disclosure

a. The Corporation publishes its sponsor relationship status on its website during the sponsorship period.

b. The Annual Report (during sponsorship years) reports sponsor fees as a separate line item.

c. The sponsor agreement is not published (typically contains commercial-confidence terms), but the existence and basic terms are publicly summarized.

## Open questions

- **Direct file without sponsorship.** Strong argument exists for skipping fiscal sponsorship entirely, filing 1023 immediately after incorporation, and relying on the 27-month retroactive window for donations during the determination wait. This avoids 6–8% leakage and the donor-confusion of a sponsor relationship. **Recommend deciding before incorporation, not after.**
- **Sponsor selection if needed.** PPF is the leading candidate. Verify current fee structure and any new project capacity constraints in May 2026.
- **Crypto donations through sponsor.** Sponsors handle crypto differently. PPF accepts crypto via The Giving Block. HCB has native crypto support. Material if HAND expects crypto inflows.
- **Sovereign Companions IP under sponsorship.** If HAND develops Sovereign Companions during the sponsorship period, who owns the methodology and code? Address in sponsor agreement: HAND retains all IP, sponsor has no claim. Critical.

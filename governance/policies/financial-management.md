# Financial Management and Internal Controls Policy

> Establishes the financial control framework for HAND Protocol Foundation: segregation of duties, signing authorities, reserves, audit and review thresholds, and treatment of restricted funds.

**Status:** Draft v0.1 · May 2026 · Pre-adoption · Pending counsel review

---

## Section 1. Purpose

This Policy establishes the financial management framework of the Corporation to ensure:

a. Accurate and reliable financial records.

b. Safeguarding of Corporation assets.

c. Compliance with law, donor restrictions, grant terms, and the Bylaws.

d. Adequate internal control to prevent and detect errors and misappropriation.

e. Sound stewardship that meets the expectations of funders and the public.

## Section 2. Authority and roles

a. **Board of Directors.** Approves the annual budget; approves capital expenditures above $25,000; approves any new credit line, loan, or material contract above $50,000; receives and reviews quarterly financial reports.

b. **Treasurer.** Oversees financial affairs; chairs the Finance Committee; signs the Form 990; ensures internal-controls policy is followed; provides regular Board-meeting financial reports.

c. **Finance Committee.** Reviews monthly financial reports; reviews the proposed annual budget; reviews investment performance and policy; reviews any internal-control exceptions; engages external CPAs.

d. **Audit Committee** (combined with Finance until separated). Engages and oversees the external auditor when applicable; reviews the audit report and any management letter; meets at least once with the auditor without management present.

e. **Executive Director.** Manages day-to-day financial operations; ensures financial records are accurate; ensures internal controls are followed; provides financial information to the Treasurer and Finance Committee.

f. **Bookkeeper / Accountant** (initially likely a contractor). Maintains the books; processes payables and receivables; reconciles accounts; prepares monthly financial statements.

g. **CPA firm.** Performs annual compilation, review, or audit per Section 8. Prepares Form 990. Provides tax advice.

## Section 3. Segregation of duties

Where staff size permits, the following functions shall be separated:

a. **Authorization** (who approves a transaction).

b. **Recording** (who enters the transaction in the books).

c. **Custody** (who holds the cash or asset).

d. **Reconciliation** (who reconciles the books against external evidence).

In the early stage where staff is one or two people, **the Treasurer assumes the reconciliation role** to provide independent oversight. As soon as a third staff person is added, the Treasurer's reconciliation responsibility can be delegated to that staff person, with the Treasurer reviewing.

## Section 4. Signing authorities

a. **Single signature** is authorized for transactions up to **$5,000**. Single-signature checks may be signed by the Executive Director or the Treasurer.

b. **Dual signature** is required for transactions from **$5,000 to $25,000**. Dual signatures are any two of: Executive Director, Treasurer, Board Chair.

c. **Dual signature plus board resolution** for transactions over **$25,000**. The board resolution may be a standing resolution (e.g., approval of an annual budget line) or a transaction-specific resolution.

d. **Wire transfers** above $5,000 require dual approval regardless of amount. Wire transfer authorization shall use call-back verification for any new payee.

e. **Credit and debit cards.** Each cardholder is responsible for documenting all charges with receipts within 30 days. No personal use of Corporation cards. No cash advances. No payments to vendors not previously approved.

f. **Signing authority list** is maintained by the Secretary and provided to the bank. Changes require board resolution.

## Section 5. Banking

a. **Bank selection.** Banking relationship with a regulated U.S. financial institution. Preference for community development financial institutions (CDFIs) where service levels permit.

b. **Account structure.** Separate accounts for: operating, restricted/grant funds, and reserves. Restricted funds in interest-bearing accounts to maximize charitable yield.

c. **Reconciliation.** All bank accounts reconciled monthly by the bookkeeper, with reconciliation reviewed by the Treasurer (or, when delegated, an independent third staff person).

d. **Online banking.** Two-factor authentication required for all accounts. Authorized users limited to the Executive Director and Treasurer.

## Section 6. Budgeting

a. **Annual budget.** Drafted by the Executive Director and Treasurer; reviewed by the Finance Committee; approved by the Board no later than the December meeting for the following fiscal year.

b. **Budget structure.** Reflects HAND's program structure: foundation operations, Reciprocate program, Sovereign Reciprocates program, fundraising, and management & general.

c. **Variance review.** Quarterly variance reports comparing actuals to budget. Variances >10% on any line item require explanation to the Finance Committee.

d. **Mid-year revision.** A mid-year budget revision is performed if material conditions change (new grant won or lost, founding ED hired, major program scope change).

## Section 7. Reserves

a. **Target reserve.** Three to six months of operating expenses, accumulated over the first five years.

b. **Reserve account.** Held in a separate account (Section 5.b). Withdrawals require Board resolution unless triggered by an emergency provision below.

c. **Emergency withdrawal.** The Executive Director may direct withdrawal from reserves up to **one month of operating expenses** in an emergency (e.g., delayed grant payment causing cash crunch), with notification to the Board Chair and Treasurer within 24 hours. Board ratification required at the next meeting.

d. **Investment.** Reserves above a 30-day operating cushion may be invested per a Board-adopted Investment Policy (not yet drafted; pending growth of reserves beyond $50,000).

## Section 8. External financial review

External CPA engagement scales with revenue and funder expectation:

| Annual gross receipts | Minimum external work | When |
|---|---|---|
| < $250,000 | **Compilation** | Required by board policy; not required by IRS |
| $250,000 – $499,999 | **Compilation** (Review optional if funder requires) | |
| $500,000 – $999,999 | **Review** | Required by board policy |
| ≥ $1,000,000 | **Audit** | Required by some institutional funders regardless of revenue |
| Any level if a funder requires | **As required** | |

A **compilation** is the lightest CPA engagement (no assurance). A **review** provides limited assurance. An **audit** provides reasonable assurance and is the most rigorous (and expensive: $15,000–$30,000 for a small nonprofit).

The CPA firm may not be replaced more than once in any five-year period without explicit Audit Committee deliberation, to avoid the appearance of audit shopping.

## Section 9. Restricted, temporarily restricted, and unrestricted funds

a. **Unrestricted.** Donations and grants without donor-imposed use restrictions. May be used for any purpose consistent with HAND's exempt purpose.

b. **Temporarily restricted (purpose- or time-restricted).** Donor-restricted to a specific program, project, or time period. Tracked separately. Released to unrestricted when the restriction is met. Reported on Statement of Activities under ASC 958.

c. **Permanently restricted (endowment).** Not currently anticipated. If accepted, requires Board-adopted endowment policy and FASB-compliant accounting.

d. **Recording.** Restrictions documented at time of receipt, in the acknowledgment letter, and in the books.

e. **Pivots.** If a restricted grant cannot be used as originally restricted, the Executive Director shall consult the donor before reallocating, in writing. **Self-modifying restrictions** is a fast path to a public-charity status problem.

## Section 10. Fundraising costs and ratios

a. **Fundraising efficiency.** HAND aspires to a fundraising-cost-to-funds-raised ratio of no more than **20%** (i.e., $0.20 of every $1.00 raised goes to fundraising) at organizational maturity, and reports this ratio in the Annual Report.

b. **Program-vs-overhead ratio.** HAND rejects the "overhead myth" framing pushed by Charity Navigator, GuideStar, and BBB Wise Giving Alliance in their joint 2013 letter. Program ratio is one signal among many, not a quality measure. HAND will report the ratio because Form 990 requires it, but will not optimize for it at the expense of investing in capacity.

c. **Joint costs.** Costs that are partly fundraising and partly program (e.g., a publication that solicits donations and also serves educational purposes) are allocated per AICPA Statement of Position 98-2. Documented allocation method.

## Section 11. In-kind and non-cash contributions

a. **Recording.** In-kind goods and services with a fair market value over $1,000 are recorded as both revenue and expense in the books, at fair market value.

b. **Volunteer time.** Professional services contributed by Contributors are recorded as in-kind only when the services would otherwise have been purchased and the Contributor has the relevant professional credentials, per FASB ASC 958-605.

c. **Donor acknowledgment.** In-kind donors receive an acknowledgment that thanks them and describes the gift, but does **not** state a value. The donor is responsible for substantiation if claiming a deduction.

d. **Schedule M and Form 8283.** Triggered when non-cash contributions exceed $25,000/year and for individual gifts over $5,000. See [`gift-acceptance.md`](gift-acceptance.md).

## Section 12. Payroll and contractors

a. **Worker classification.** Each engagement is reviewed for employee vs independent contractor classification under IRS 20-factor test and Department of Labor economic-realities test. Misclassification is a material risk.

b. **Payroll.** Outsourced to a reputable payroll provider (Gusto, ADP, or equivalent) from the first hire. The provider handles tax withholding, deposits, and W-2 issuance.

c. **Contractors.** File Form 1099-NEC for each contractor whose cumulative payments reach the annual reporting threshold; collect a W-9 before first payment; apply 24% backup withholding (Form 945) if a valid TIN isn't furnished. **Threshold note (updated 2026-06-01):** the figure was historically $600; the OBBBA (P.L. 119-21) raised the 1099-NEC threshold to **$2,000 for payments made in 2026**, inflation-adjusted thereafter — confirm the current figure at filing time. See the `/hand-tax` skill (Mode C) for worker classification and the cited brief for sources.

> **Note — unrelated business income (Develop pillar).** Service revenue from selling web/SEO/ecommerce to outside businesses is almost certainly **unrelated business income** (Form 990-T at $1,000 gross, 21% on net), and the web work is a **taxable Texas data-processing service** requiring sales-tax collection. This is owned by the `/hand-tax` skill (Modes A, G) and gated on counsel before the first invoice; it is not a payroll matter and is cross-referenced here only so the bookkeeper flags it.

d. **Time tracking.** Salaried staff exempt from FLSA time tracking, except for grant-allocation purposes if any grant requires effort certification.

## Section 13. Travel and expense reimbursement

a. **Accountable plan** under IRS Pub. 463. Reimbursements made under this plan are not taxable to the recipient.

b. **Submission.** Expense reports with receipts within 60 days of incurring the expense. Per-mile reimbursement at the federal standard rate.

c. **Per diem (optional).** May use federal per diem rates for meals and incidentals on overnight travel, in lieu of itemized receipts.

## Section 14. Insurance

a. **Directors and Officers (D&O) liability** before any board service begins.

b. **General liability** before any in-person program activity, public event, or in-person Reciprocate or Contributor engagement.

c. **Cyber liability** within the first year of operations or before holding any Reciprocate-group data, whichever is sooner.

d. **Workers' compensation** if and when the first paid employee is hired (Texas allows opt-out for nonprofits, but coverage is strongly recommended).

e. **Crime / fidelity bond** within the first year, sized to one month's average cash position.

f. **Renewal review.** The Finance Committee reviews policies and quotes annually.

## Section 15. Investments

Pending a separate Investment Policy adoption when reserves exceed $50,000. Until then:

a. **Operating cash** in FDIC-insured deposit accounts.

b. **Reserves** in high-yield savings, money market, or short-duration U.S. Treasury bills.

c. **No speculative investments.** No cryptocurrency holdings other than donations received and converted to USD per the Gift Acceptance Policy. No equity, derivatives, or alternative assets.

## Section 16. Internal control failures and corrections

a. Any internal control failure observed (single-signature on an over-threshold check, missing receipt, etc.) is logged by the bookkeeper.

b. The Treasurer reviews the log quarterly. Patterns trigger procedural changes.

c. **Material failure** (any single event with potential loss >$10,000 or any pattern indicating systematic weakness) is reported to the Audit Committee within 7 days and to the full Board at the next meeting.

## Open questions

- **CDFI banking partner.** Texas options include LiftFund (San Antonio), PeopleFund (Austin), and BCL of Texas. Worth a relationship even if HAND's checking is at a more conventional bank early on.
- **Payroll provider.** Gusto is the standard small-nonprofit choice ($40/mo + $6/employee). Onpay and Patriot are cheaper alternatives. Defer until first hire.
- **D&O insurance carrier.** Nonprofits Insurance Alliance (NIA), Hartford, Philadelphia Insurance, USLI. Recommend NIA for nonprofit specialization. Quote pre-incorporation; bind day-of-incorporation.
- **Reserve target.** 3-6 months drafted. Some sector experts recommend 9-12 months for relational, accompaniment-style orgs because revenue is lumpy. Revisit in year three.
- **Investment policy.** Defer until reserves are big enough to invest. Mission-aligned investing options (Tides Foundation impact pool, RSF Social Finance) worth considering at that point.

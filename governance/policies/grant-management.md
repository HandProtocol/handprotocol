# Grant Management Policy

> Governs the lifecycle of grants received by HAND Protocol Foundation: identification, application, acceptance, drawdown, reporting, and closeout. Complements the Gift Acceptance Policy (which covers donations broadly) and the Financial Management Policy (which covers internal controls).

**Status:** Draft v0.1 · May 2026 · Pre-adoption

---

## Section 1. Purpose

Grants are a core revenue source for HAND Protocol Foundation. This Policy establishes the procedures by which the Corporation identifies grant opportunities, applies, manages awarded grants, reports to funders, and closes out grants in a way that:

a. Preserves donor intent and restriction.

b. Avoids over-promising and under-delivering, which damages funder trust.

c. Maintains compliant financial tracking of restricted funds.

d. Aligns grant-seeking with the strategic plan rather than chasing dollars opportunistically.

## Section 2. Grant-seeking alignment

Before pursuing any grant opportunity, the Executive Director (or designee) shall consider:

a. **Mission alignment.** Does this grant fund work HAND is already doing or planning to do under the strategic plan?

b. **Capacity reality.** Can HAND deliver what the grant funds at the proposed scale and timeline without burning out staff or compromising existing commitments?

c. **Fit with funder.** Is the funder's stated portfolio, voice, and grantee profile consistent with HAND's positioning?

d. **Strings attached.** Does the grant carry restrictions, reporting burdens, or naming requirements that would compromise HAND's brand or sovereignty commitments?

e. **Total cost of pursuit.** Some grant applications take 40+ hours of senior staff time. Is the expected value worth that investment?

**Mission-aligned, capacity-honest, and not desperate.** HAND will decline grants that would distort the work, even if the dollars are real.

## Section 3. Grant pipeline management

a. **Pipeline tracker.** Maintained per [`../grants/funder-pipeline.md`](../grants/funder-pipeline.md). Categories: prospects, active applications, awarded, declined, declined-to-pursue.

b. **Quarterly pipeline review** by Finance Committee, including:
- Prospects identified in the prior quarter.
- Applications submitted.
- Awards and declines.
- Pipeline-conversion rate (a board-watched metric).

c. **Annual fundraising plan** drafted by the Executive Director with the Finance Committee; approved by the Board as part of the annual budget.

## Section 4. Application process

a. **Letter of Inquiry (LOI).** Where the funder accepts LOIs, an LOI is the standard first step. Reviewed by the Executive Director and one Board member before submission.

b. **Full application.** Drafted by the Executive Director with subject-matter input from program staff. Major applications (>$50,000 ask) reviewed by the Board Chair before submission. Applications above $100,000 require Board approval before submission.

c. **Application file.** For each application, a file is maintained containing:
- Funder name, contact, deadline.
- Ask amount, project scope, budget submitted.
- Final submitted application and attachments.
- Submission confirmation.
- Correspondence with funder.
- Award notification or decline.

d. **No double-counting.** A single project budget line is committed to one funder. HAND will not solicit overlapping funding for the same expenses from multiple funders unless explicitly allowed (some funders require this disclosure on the application).

## Section 5. Award acceptance

a. **Award letter review.** Each grant award letter is reviewed by the Executive Director and Treasurer to confirm:
- Award amount, restrictions, period of performance.
- Reporting requirements (narrative and financial; frequency).
- Branding and recognition requirements.
- Audit or financial-review requirements.
- Any unusual terms (matching requirements, claw-back provisions, indemnification, IP assignment).

b. **Board notice.** Awards over $25,000 are reported to the Board at the next meeting. Awards over $100,000 require Board acceptance by resolution.

c. **Grant Agreement execution.** Signed by the Executive Director under the signing-authority policy. Counter-signed by the funder.

d. **Acknowledgment letter** sent within 14 days of receipt of funds.

## Section 6. Restricted funds accounting

a. **Setup.** Each restricted grant is set up as a separate fund / class in the accounting system at award.

b. **Budget allocation.** A project budget is allocated to the fund, matching the application budget unless explicitly renegotiated with the funder.

c. **Expense tracking.** Every expense charged against the grant is coded to the fund and to a budget category (personnel, contractors, supplies, travel, etc.).

d. **Release from restriction.** As expenses are incurred against the grant, the restriction is released for the corresponding amount, moving the funds from "temporarily restricted" to "unrestricted" net assets per FASB ASC 958.

e. **Time restrictions.** Multi-year grants are recognized as revenue in full at award (when restriction is met by performance over time), with the time-restricted portion shown as a liability or as net-assets-with-donor-restrictions on the Statement of Financial Position.

## Section 7. Drawdown discipline

a. **Match expenses to budget.** Expenses charged to a grant must reasonably match the budget category for that grant.

b. **Budget revisions.** If actual expenses will deviate materially from budget (>10% on any major category), the Executive Director shall request budget revision approval from the funder in writing before incurring the variant expense. Most funders permit modest reallocations; some require pre-approval for any change.

c. **Costs after performance period.** Expenses incurred after the end of the period of performance are not chargeable to the grant absent explicit funder approval.

d. **Pre-award costs.** Generally not allowed. If pre-award costs are necessary (e.g., to start work immediately on a funder-accelerated timeline), confirm in writing with the funder before incurring.

e. **Cost sharing / match.** Where the grant requires matching funds, the match is tracked separately with documentation (in-kind valuations, other-funder commitments).

f. **Indirect / overhead allocation.** Where the funder allows an indirect-cost rate, HAND charges it. Where the funder caps indirect, HAND tracks the gap to general operations. Where the funder disallows indirect, HAND considers whether the grant is worth pursuing.

## Section 8. Reporting to funders

a. **Reporting calendar.** Each grant's reporting deadlines are calendared at award acceptance. Reminders 30 and 7 days before each deadline.

b. **Narrative report content.** Aligned with the grant proposal: activities, milestones, outcomes, lessons learned, challenges, plans for next period.

c. **Financial report content.** Aligned with the proposal budget: actuals to date, remaining balance, variance explanations.

d. **Reporting voice.** Honest about both progress and shortfalls. Funders trust grantees who acknowledge challenges before being asked.

e. **Report file.** Each report submitted is filed with the grant file.

f. **Late reports.** Notify funder in advance if a deadline will be missed. Late reports without notice are a fast path to grantee-watch-list status.

## Section 9. Stewardship beyond reporting

a. **Funder updates between deadlines.** Major milestones, public recognition opportunities, and material changes are communicated to the funder outside formal reporting.

b. **Site visits.** When funders offer to visit, HAND welcomes and prepares thoughtfully: agenda, time with staff and Reciprocates (with Reciprocate consent), and documentation of the visit.

c. **Convenings.** When funders host learning convenings, HAND staff and (where appropriate) Reciprocates or Contributors attend. Insights brought back to the team.

d. **Long-game cultivation.** Reporting and stewardship are the relationship-building substrate for future funding cycles. Each grant is treated as the start of an ongoing relationship, not a transaction.

## Section 10. Closeout

a. **Final report.** Submitted on the deadline specified in the grant agreement.

b. **Final financial report.** Reconciles total spend against budget, with documentation of any unspent funds.

c. **Unspent funds.** Returned to the funder per the grant agreement terms, OR proposed for redirection with funder approval. **HAND does not unilaterally retain unspent restricted funds.**

d. **Asset disposition.** Equipment or property purchased with grant funds is treated per the grant agreement's asset-disposition clause (some funders require return or specific reuse; many allow continued use for exempt purpose).

e. **Records.** Grant file retained per the Document Retention Policy (7 years after final report acceptance, with permanent retention of executive summary and award letter as part of board-relevant history).

f. **Public acknowledgment.** Funder publicly acknowledged in the Annual Report covering the grant period, and on the website's funders page (with funder permission).

## Section 11. Special situations

a. **Grant disputes.** Disagreements with funder over interpretation of grant terms are escalated to the Executive Director and (if material) to the Board Chair. Disputes are addressed in good faith and in writing.

b. **Funder request to change scope.** Requests to redirect mid-grant are evaluated against HAND's mission. HAND retains the right to decline a funder-requested change that conflicts with HAND's values or strategic plan, returning unspent funds if necessary.

c. **Funder reputational issues.** If a funder becomes the subject of reputational concern materially after award, the Board reviews. Default is to complete the funded engagement and decline future cycles, rather than refund mid-engagement.

d. **Pass-through grants.** HAND may, at Board discretion, sub-grant to other organizations or Reciprocate groups under terms aligned with HAND's mission. Sub-grants require written sub-grant agreements with the same reporting and closeout discipline as upstream grants.

## Section 12. Sovereign Reciprocates program grants

Special provisions for grants to or for the Sovereign Reciprocates program:

a. **Adapter ownership preserved.** No grant accepted may transfer or restrict ownership of Reciprocate-group-owned adapters, training data, or evaluation logs to the funder.

b. **Open-source release commitment honored.** Grants to the program assume the program's open-source posture; HAND will not accept a grant requiring the methodology, code, or eval framework to be proprietary.

c. **Reciprocate consent.** Grants funding work with specific Reciprocate groups require those groups' written consent to be named (or anonymized) in funder reports.

d. **Funder convenings.** Reciprocate groups may attend funder convenings about their own work, at HAND's expense, with their consent.

## Open questions

- **Pipeline-conversion rate target.** No good industry benchmark. Recommend tracking but not setting a target until 12 months of data exists.
- **Indirect cost rate.** HAND has no negotiated indirect cost rate with any funder. Federal grants would require one (NICRA). For now, indirect is built into program budgets as allocated lines.
- **Sub-granting infrastructure.** If HAND begins sub-granting to Reciprocate groups (a possible expansion of the program), a sub-grant agreement template is required. Defer until needed.
- **Funder due-diligence package.** Standard 15-item packet maintained per [`../grants/standard-attachments-checklist.md`](../grants/standard-attachments-checklist.md). Keep current.

# Board Outreach Templates

> The email and message templates for inviting prospective directors to a first conversation. Hand-tailor every send; never use these verbatim. They exist so you don't start from blank page.

**Status:** Draft v0.1 · May 2026 · Living document

---

## How to use these

a. **Personalize.** Every outreach mentions something specific to the recipient: their work, a piece they wrote, a connection, a project. If you can't think of a specific reason, don't send.

b. **Lead with respect.** Their time is the scarce resource. Make the ask concrete and easy to refuse.

c. **One ask per outreach.** Either "would you take a conversation" or "would you consider joining the board." Not both.

d. **Brief is better.** A board ask under 150 words feels respectful. Over 400 words feels heavy.

e. **Honor "no" and "not now."** The relationship matters; the seat is just one moment. Many strong directors say yes the second time.

f. **Direct the reader to the brief.** The [Director Prospect Brief](director-prospect-brief.md) carries the detail; the outreach carries the relational opening.

## Template 1: Cold email to a prospect introduced by a mutual connection

**Subject:** [Mutual connection's name] suggested I reach out

> Hi [Name],
>
> [Mutual connection] mentioned you might be the right person to talk to about something we're building. I'd appreciate your read.
>
> I'm the founder of HAND Protocol Foundation. We're incorporating as a 501(c)(3) in Austin this year. The work is long-term operational accompaniment for healers, impact entrepreneurs, and grassroots community organizations: the layer below the radar of the existing capacity-building ecosystem.
>
> [Specific reason you'd want them on the board, drawn from their work. Two to three sentences. Reference a piece they wrote, a project they led, an alignment that's not obvious to a stranger.]
>
> We're seating the founding board this year. I'd like to know if a 45-minute conversation about whether HAND might be a fit for your next board role would be welcome.
>
> The 2-pager on what we're asking of directors is here: [link to handprotocol.org/governance/board-prospect].
>
> Either way, glad [mutual connection] introduced us.
>
> [Founder name]
> hand@handprotocol.org · handprotocol.org

**Sender notes:**

- 150–200 words.
- The "specific reason" sentence is the load-bearing piece. Get it right.
- Link the brief instead of pasting it.
- Reply within 5 business days even to a no.

## Template 2: Warm outreach to someone you already know

**Subject:** A board ask, take your time

> [Name],
>
> Quick note. We're seating the founding board for HAND Protocol Foundation this year and you came to mind. The work is described at handprotocol.org. The 2-pager on what's involved is at handprotocol.org/governance/board-prospect.
>
> Three reasons I'm thinking of you specifically: [first reason]; [second reason]; [third reason].
>
> 6 to 10 hours a month, three-year term. Founding-board level of engagement. We make no compensation for service.
>
> If a conversation feels right, my calendar is at [Calendly or similar link]. If it doesn't, no pressure, and please share if anyone else comes to mind.
>
> [Founder name]

**Sender notes:**

- Under 120 words.
- Warm channels let you skip more setup.
- The three-reasons is the substance.

**Sender notes:**

## Template 4: Re-engagement after "not now"

**Subject:** A check-in

> [Name],
>
> Hope the year has been good. We talked [X months] ago about HAND's founding board. Timing wasn't right; I appreciate you being honest about that.
>
> Two updates that might be relevant:
>
> [Concrete update 1. What HAND has built, who's joined, what's been published.]
>
> [Concrete update 2.]
>
> If circumstances have shifted on your end, the seat is still open. If not, still happy you're in the orbit.
>
> [Founder name]

**Sender notes:**

- 100–130 words.
- Update them on what's changed; don't repitch.
- 6-9 months after a "not now" is a typical re-engagement window.

## Template 5: Companion or Contributor as prospective director

> [Name],
>
> Different kind of ask this time. Our governance work is producing a board, and I'd like to know if you'd consider a seat.
>
> The thinking: your relationship with HAND so far has been [Companion / Contributor]. You know the work from the inside. You'd bring [specific lived experience or perspective] that the board needs. Companion or Contributor representation on the founding board would change the quality of the deliberations.
>
> There are some real things to think through: the conflict-of-interest implications, the workload alongside your own work, the way it would change our current relationship.
>
> Would you take a conversation on whether this is right? No commitment, just exploring.
>
> The brief: handprotocol.org/governance/board-prospect.

**Sender notes:**

- Companion or Contributor on the board is a major fit and a real complication. Conflict-of-interest analysis required.
- This conversation should happen later in HAND's life (Year 2-3), not at founding, unless the Companion or Contributor pre-dates the founding.

## Template 6: Asking for referrals (not direct prospecting)

**Subject:** Three names

> [Name],
>
> Not asking you to join the board (you've got enough on your plate). Asking for your thinking.
>
> We're seating HAND's founding board and have specific gaps: [gap 1], [gap 2], [gap 3]. The full Composition Matrix is at [link].
>
> Three names that come to mind? Even one would help. Warm intro not necessary; happy to cold-reach if you'd flag a name.
>
> Pre-thanks.
>
> [Founder name]

**Sender notes:**

- Under 80 words.
- "Three names" is more actionable than "any thoughts?"
- Follow up on every name they give, and tell them the outcome.

## What NOT to send

a. **Boilerplate that doesn't say why this person.** Generic asks read as low-effort and weaken the project's seriousness.

b. **The full brief in the email.** Send the link. Force the reader to make a small effort; it filters for engagement.

c. **A "we need" framing.** The recipient is not your solution; they're a person being asked to consider something. Frame as "we'd value your read."

d. **Multiple asks layered.** "Join the board" + "also could you connect us to X funder" + "could you donate", pick one. Layer over time.

e. **Heroic narratives.** "We're changing the world" reads thin and HAND-off-brand. Plain English and specifics.

f. **Urgency manufactured.** "We need a decision by Friday" forces a no. Real deadlines, communicated honestly, when they exist.

---

## Cadence and tracking

a. **Track in the funder pipeline tracker** or a separate Board Outreach tracker.

b. **5-day response SLA** on incoming responses, even to a no.

c. **Follow-up on silence at 7 days** with a soft re-touch, then let it go.

d. **Quarterly review** of warm-but-not-yet prospects.

e. **Closure note** at the end of every conversation: thank you, here's what happens next, here's how I'll stay in touch.

## Open questions

- **Calendly or manual scheduling.** Calendly works but feels less personal. For board prospects, manual scheduling is often the right signal in the first conversation.
- **The brief at handprotocol.org/governance/board-prospect.** Will go live with the governance section of the website.
- **Tracking system.** Google Sheet sufficient for Year 1; HubSpot Nonprofit or Salesforce NPSP later.

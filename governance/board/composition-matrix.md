# Board Composition Matrix

> The tool the Governance Committee uses to plan board recruitment: what skills, lived experience, networks, and identity perspectives the board has, what it needs, and where the gaps are.

**Status:** Draft v0.1 · May 2026 · Pre-adoption · Updated annually

---

## How to use this document

The Composition Matrix is the Governance Committee's primary recruitment instrument. It is **not** a quota system. It is a map of what HAND's board would benefit from having, used to guide recruitment so the board reflects the work, the population served, and the funder ecosystem.

The Matrix is **reviewed annually** at the year-end Governance Committee meeting and updated when directors join, leave, or change roles.

---

## Section 1. Target board composition

For HAND's organizational stage:

- **Year 1 (founding):** 3 directors minimum (Texas BOC §22.204).
- **Year 2:** 5 directors target.
- **Year 3 and beyond:** 7 directors target, expandable to 9 as growth warrants.
- **Maximum** per Bylaws: 15.

The target reflects research that small-org effectiveness peaks at 5-7 active directors. Above 7, marginal contributions decline; below 5, the board lacks resilience and diversity of perspective.

| **Impact entrepreneurs** (post-accelerator, social enterprise) | Reciprocate segment 2 |

| **LGBTQ+ perspectives** | Many Reciprocate groups serve these communities |
| **Disability perspectives** | Accessibility is mission-aligned; disability community lived experience strengthens decisions |
| **Texas / Central Texas roots** | Home base; community foundation funders prioritize local leadership |

| **Class diversity** | First-generation, working-class, and wealth perspectives all matter |

## Section 6. Gap analysis

Updated annually. Identifies the top 3-5 gaps and frames the recruitment priorities for the year.

Example (illustrative, not actual):

**Top gaps (Year 1):**

1. **Treasurer-qualified director with CPA/CFO background.** Most urgent. Without this, financial oversight is dependent on the founder, which weakens internal control and limits the rebuttable-presumption procedure.

2. **Lived experience as Reciprocate.** Founding board may be missing direct connection to one or more of the three Reciprocate segments. Recruit specifically.

## Section 7. Recruitment principles

HAND recruits board members as follows:

a. **Mission alignment is non-negotiable.** A candidate strong on skills but indifferent to mission is not a fit.

b. **Time commitment honest.** Candidates are told the 6-10 hour/month expectation and the 100% board giving expectation up front.

c. **Reciprocate / Contributor invitation friendly.** Where appropriate, recruit from HAND's own community. Mind the conflict-of-interest implications.

d. **No board-by-prestige.** A famous name without active engagement weakens governance. HAND prefers active over notable.

e. **Slow over fast.** Better to leave a seat open than fill it with the wrong person.

f. **Active recruiting, not passive intake.** Targeted outreach based on the gap analysis.

g. **Pipeline of interest, not just open recruitment.** Maintain an ongoing list of candidates and warm them slowly.

## Section 8. Recruitment process

a. **Identification.** Through staff, board, Contributors, Reciprocates, peer organizations, board placement services (BoardSource, BoardBuild, BoardLead).

b. **First conversation** with the Vice Chair (Governance Committee Chair) or Board Chair: discuss mission, role, expectations.

c. **Mutual fit conversation** with the Executive Director and one other director.

d. **Reference checks** for any candidate from outside HAND's immediate community.

e. **Recommendation** by the Governance Committee to the full Board.

f. **Election** by majority vote of the Board at a regular or special meeting.

g. **Onboarding** within 30 days of election: orientation, materials, mentor pairing.

## Section 9. Onboarding for new directors

Every new director receives:

a. **Orientation meeting** with the Board Chair and Executive Director.

b. **Governance binder** (digital and/or paper): Articles, Bylaws, all policies, Board Member Agreement, current strategic plan, theory of change, recent minutes, recent financials.

c. **Mentor pairing** with an existing director for the first 6 months.

d. **Site visits** to HAND program activity (a Reciprocate engagement, with Reciprocate consent; a Sovereign Reciprocates overview).

e. **Committee assignment** based on skills and interest.

f. **First 90 days touchpoint** with the Vice Chair / Governance Chair to address questions.

## Section 10. Board self-assessment

Annually:

a. Each director completes a self-assessment of their own engagement.

b. The Board collectively assesses board functioning.

c. Findings reviewed by the Governance Committee.

d. Improvements adopted by the Board.

Assessment tools: BoardSource Board Self-Assessment, or equivalent.

## Section 11. Term rotation planning

Per Bylaws, directors serve 3-year terms, eligible for one re-election (6 years max). Staggered terms target one-third of seats expiring annually. The Governance Committee tracks term expirations and begins recruitment 6 months before each term end.

## Open questions

- **Reciprocate / Contributor seats on board.** Strong argument for formal seats; logistical complications with conflict-of-interest and term overlap. Recommended path: add formally in year two amendment.
- **Compensated board members.** Drafted as fully volunteer. If HAND grows to a point where adding compensated Reciprocate-representative seats would be meaningful, revisit. Public-charity status implications.
- **External board placement services.** BoardSource, BoardBuild, BoardLead worth contacting once HAND has clear gap-analysis priorities.
- **Geographic distribution.** Should HAND aim for Texas-heavy board (community foundation alignment) or national board (funder reach)? Probably both, weighted toward Texas in early years.

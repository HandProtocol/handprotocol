# Succession and Replacement Procedures

> Five scenarios. Each has a documented procedure so that continuity, accountability, and HAND's mission survive any single departure or transition.

**Status:** Draft v0.1 · May 2026 · Pre-adoption · Pending counsel review

---

## Section 1. Purpose

HAND Protocol Foundation commits to operate as if it will outlive any single person. Succession and replacement procedures protect:

a. The Corporation's continuity through transitions.

b. Reciprocate-group relationships, especially the long-term accompaniment promise.

c. Sovereign Reciprocates adapters and the data sovereignty commitments embedded in them.

d. Donor trust and funder confidence.

e. Staff and Contributor well-being during inevitable transitions.

This document covers five scenarios:

1. **Emergency Executive Director succession** (sudden incapacity, death, abrupt departure).
2. **Planned Executive Director transition** (retirement, mutual decision, 6–18 month notice).
3. **Founder departure** (the founder of HAND stepping back from operational and/or board role).
4. **Board Chair succession** (planned rotation or unplanned departure).
5. **Director removal** (for-cause or no-cause, by Board action).

Each scenario has its own triggers, actors, timeline, and documentation requirements.

## Section 2. Emergency Executive Director succession

### Trigger

Sudden incapacity, death, unexpected resignation, or removal of the Executive Director with less than 30 days' notice.

### Immediate actors (first 48 hours)

a. **Board Chair** convenes an emergency meeting of the Executive Committee within 24 hours of notification.

b. **Treasurer** confirms access to financial systems and confirms signing authority for the next 90 days.

c. **Secretary** preserves all records and ensures the incident is documented.

d. **Acting ED** (see designation below) assumes operational authority immediately upon Board confirmation.

### Acting ED designation

The Acting ED is designated in the following order of preference:

1. **The Board-designated Acting ED**, named in advance and updated annually by the Board. Default candidates: the Board Chair, a designated Vice Chair, or a senior staff person.

2. **The Board Chair**, if no advance designation is current.

3. **A staff member chosen by majority vote of the Executive Committee**, where the Chair is also unavailable.

The Acting ED has **full operational authority for up to 90 days**, with the following limitations requiring Board approval:
- New hires.
- Terminations beyond routine.
- Contracts above $25,000.
- Material program changes.
- Public statements on behalf of the Corporation.

### Communications plan (first 72 hours)

a. **Staff and Contributors** notified within 24 hours, in person where possible, with a brief written summary and reassurance about operations.

b. **Board** notified within 24 hours by the Board Chair.

c. **Funders and major donors** notified within 48 hours by the Board Chair and the Acting ED. The message: who is in charge, what is continuing, what to expect.

d. **Reciprocates and Reciprocate groups** notified within 72 hours by the Acting ED with personal touch, especially for active engagements. The message: HAND is continuing the engagement; here is the new point of contact.

e. **Public statement** issued by 72 hours if the situation warrants public disclosure. Drafted by the Board Chair and the Acting ED.

### Board Response Committee

Within 7 days of the incident, the Board appoints a **Board Response Committee** of 3 to 5 members to:

a. Oversee the Acting ED arrangement.

b. Begin search and selection for a permanent replacement (or interim ED appointment if appropriate).

c. Communicate with the staff, board, and major stakeholders.

d. Manage any legal, financial, or reputational fallout.

### Cross-trained backup matrix

The Corporation maintains a **Cross-Trained Backup Matrix** ([`emergency-succession-plan.md`](emergency-succession-plan.md)) identifying, for each critical function, the primary owner and at least one backup who can perform the function in an emergency:

- Banking and finance signatures
- Payroll and vendor payments
- Donor communications
- Funder communications
- Major grant reporting deadlines
- Reciprocate engagement continuity (per Reciprocate)
- Contributor pool management
- Sovereign Reciprocates system access and operation
- Website, email, and social-media access
- Legal hold compliance
- Insurance broker contact

The Matrix is reviewed quarterly by the Treasurer and the ED and updated whenever a position changes.

### Interim or permanent search

a. **Interim ED** strongly recommended if the permanent search will take more than 90 days. An interim ED with no candidacy for the permanent role can stabilize and reform.

b. **Permanent search** initiated by the Board Response Committee. Timeline 4–9 months typical for a small org.

c. **Selection process** consistent with Equal Opportunity and Anti-Harassment Policy. External search firm engaged if budget permits.

d. **Compensation set** under the Executive Compensation Policy and the rebuttable-presumption procedure.

### Documentation

Every step of the emergency response is documented:

- Notification timeline and recipients.
- Decisions made by the Acting ED and the Board Response Committee.
- Communications sent.
- Records preserved.

Documentation retained permanently in board records.

## Section 3. Planned Executive Director transition

### Trigger

Voluntary departure of the Executive Director with notice of 6 to 18 months. May be retirement, mutual decision, career transition, or strategic agreement.

### Transition Committee

The Board Chair appoints a Transition Committee of 3 to 5 members at the time of notice. Members include at least one director, ideally a staff representative or Contributor representative, and may include an external advisor.

### Transition timeline

| Phase | Timeframe | Activities |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery** | First 30 days | Strategic review: what does the org need now vs when current ED was hired? Job description revision. |
| **Search** | 60–180 days | Posting, outreach, interviews, references, finalists. |
| **Selection** | Days 180–210 | Board approval; compensation set; offer extended; acceptance. |
| **Onboarding overlap** | 30–60 days before start | Departing ED and incoming ED overlap. Transition documents. Stakeholder introductions. |
| **First 90 days** | After start | Board Chair partnership; check-ins at 30, 60, 90 days. |

### Interim ED option

For longer searches, a 3–9 month interim ED is recommended. Interim ED is not a candidate for the permanent role, allowing them to make hard calls without conflict.

### Donor and Reciprocate communication

a. **Departing ED's role** includes explicit handoff introductions to major donors, funders, partner organizations, and Reciprocate groups.

b. **Public message** drafted jointly with the departing ED, the Board Chair, and the incoming ED. Honest about the transition, appreciative of contributions, confident about continuity.

c. **Reciprocate-specific continuity plan** ensures each active Reciprocate group has a clear point of contact through the transition.

### Documentation

- Transition Committee minutes.
- Strategic review and job description.
- Search records (decisions, finalists, reference checks).
- Offer letter and compensation memo with rebuttable-presumption documentation.
- Departing ED's handoff documents.
- Incoming ED's 90-day onboarding plan.

## Section 4. Founder departure

### Context

The founder of HAND occupies a uniquely sensitive position in the Corporation's life. The founder's identity, voice, and relationships built the Corporation. Founder transitions are the hardest succession events; mishandled, they fracture organizations.

### Anticipating the transition

a. **From year one**, the Board treats founder transition as a real future event, not a remote possibility.

b. **Brand decoupling work** is performed deliberately during the founder's last 18 months: HAND's voice, public face, and relationships are gradually distributed across the team.

c. **Founder's relationships handed off** to other staff, board, and (where appropriate) other Contributors. Donor relationships specifically are introduced to the next ED or Board Chair well in advance.

d. **Successor's metrics reset** by the Board. The next ED is not held to founder-shaped expectations or compared unfairly.

### The mandatory off-board sabbatical

Upon founder departure from operational role (as ED or staff), the founder takes a **mandatory one-year sabbatical** from any Board role before being eligible for board service again.

**Why:** the most common founder-succession failure pattern is the founder remaining on the board and undermining the successor. The sabbatical breaks the line of authority and lets the successor establish their leadership.

### Founder-on-board specifics

If the founder remains on the Board after stepping out of operational role:

a. The founder does **not** serve as Board Chair during the transition or for at least 2 years after.

b. The founder recuses from any matter involving:
- The new ED's evaluation, compensation, or employment.
- Any program decision the new ED is responsible for.
- Founder-era contracts, vendor relationships, or commitments under reconsideration.

c. The founder commits, in writing, to the off-board sabbatical and the recusal commitments above.

### Public message

The founder's departure is communicated with intention:

a. Honest about the reasons.

b. Appreciative of the founder's contributions.

c. Confident in the new leadership.

d. Inviting continued engagement of the founder's network in the Corporation, in their new role.

e. Drafted jointly by the founder, Board Chair, and the new ED.

### Founder's continued involvement

If the founder remains involved (advisor, donor, ambassador, public voice), the involvement is:

a. Documented in writing, with scope and limits.

b. Reviewed annually by the Board.

c. Modifiable on either party's request.

### Brand and IP

a. **Brand.** The name "HAND Protocol Foundation," the visual identity, the methodologies, and the publications are the Corporation's property. The founder retains no personal IP claim on these. (Where the founder retains any pre-incorporation IP, it is documented at incorporation and licensed to the Corporation.)

b. **Personal brand.** The founder retains their own name, professional reputation, and personal writing or speaking.

## Section 5. Board Chair succession

### Planned

a. **Vice Chair pathway.** The Vice Chair is the designated successor. The Vice Chair serves a 1- to 2-year apprenticeship with the Chair, attending key conversations and gradually taking on chair responsibilities.

b. **Term.** The Chair typically serves a 1- to 3-year term, renewable once by Board vote.

c. **Election.** New Chair elected at the annual meeting following the outgoing Chair's term end.

d. **Overlap.** Incoming Chair has a defined 30–60 day overlap with the outgoing Chair.

### Unplanned

a. **Vice Chair assumes** the Chair role immediately upon any vacancy.

b. **Special election** at the next regular Board meeting to elect a new Vice Chair (and to confirm the new Chair, if the Board wishes).

### Documentation

- Election results in minutes.
- Outgoing Chair's transition memo to the incoming Chair.
- Updated officer slate filed with the bank and on internal records.

## Section 6. Director removal

### Grounds

Removal **for cause** may be based on, without limitation:

a. Material breach of fiduciary duty.

b. Prolonged unexcused absence (missing 3 consecutive regular meetings without communication, or fewer than 50% attendance in a 12-month period).

c. Material conflict of interest that cannot be managed by recusal.

d. Conduct harmful to the Corporation, its staff, Reciprocates, Contributors, or donors.

e. Material violation of the Code of Ethics or Board Member Agreement.

f. Conduct constituting a crime against the Corporation or its constituents.

g. Loss of capacity to perform Board duties.

Removal **without cause** is also permitted by Bylaws §4.6 and Texas BOC §22.211. No specific grounds are required for no-cause removal.

### Procedure

a. **Written notice** to the director, at least **seven (7) days** before the meeting at which removal will be considered. The notice includes the basis (if for cause) and the time and place of the meeting.

b. **Opportunity to be heard.** The director may appear, in person or remotely, and respond.

c. **Vote.** Removal requires the affirmative vote of **two-thirds (2/3) of the directors then in office** (excluding the director proposed for removal). The vote is taken in executive session.

d. **Documentation.** The minutes record the notice, the response (in summary), the deliberation (in summary), and the vote.

e. **Effective immediately** upon the vote, unless a later date is specified.

f. **Communication.** Removal is communicated to the rest of the Board, to staff (in summary, respecting privacy), and (if material) to funders and major donors with appropriate framing.

### Special situations

a. **Founder as removed director.** The founder is not exempt from removal procedures. Removal of the founder requires the same procedure and threshold. Heightened care in communications.

b. **Removal of Board Chair.** The Vice Chair convenes and chairs the removal meeting.

c. **Counsel involvement.** Recommended in any removal-for-cause situation. The Corporation may consult counsel before the notice is issued.

### Post-removal

a. **No retaliation** against the removed director's family or associates.

b. **Records access.** The removed director's access to Corporation records terminates upon removal. Confidentiality obligations survive.

c. **Public commentary.** The removed director's public commentary is the director's own, and HAND does not engage in public disputes. HAND may correct material factual misstatements about itself, but generally maintains a high silence.

## Section 7. Common provisions

### Documentation retention

All succession-related documents (notifications, minutes, communications, transition plans) are retained per the Document Retention Policy. Most are permanent as part of board records.

### Counsel involvement

a. **Routine transitions** do not require counsel.

b. **Emergency successions** may benefit from counsel consultation, especially regarding employment and indemnification questions.

c. **Founder transitions** benefit from counsel review of the public message and the founder's continuing arrangements.

d. **Removals for cause** generally involve counsel review of the notice and procedure.

### Communications principles

In every transition:

a. Honest about facts.

b. Considerate of all parties' dignity.

c. Appreciative of contributions where appropriate.

d. Confident about continuity.

e. Drafted with care, reviewed before sending.

### Confidentiality

Personnel matters and removal proceedings are confidential. Communications outside the necessary recipients are restricted.

### Insurance

D&O liability insurance covers directors during their service and (typically) for a tail period after departure. Confirm tail coverage at policy renewal each year.

## Section 8. Specific commitments for the founding board

For HAND's founding Board (year zero through year three):

a. **Founder serves as initial Board Chair for no more than 24 months**, after which the founder either rotates off the Chair role (remaining on the Board) or rotates off the Board entirely.

b. **Recruitment of director #4 and #5** is a Year One priority, so that the Compensation Committee can operate as a truly independent body when the founder transitions to a paid ED role (if that occurs).

c. **First succession plan review** at the end of Year One; annual review thereafter.

d. **Founder transition planning** initiated no later than the founder's third anniversary in operational role.

## Open questions

- **Founder-as-ED compensation transition.** The most likely path: founder serves unpaid through filing period, then takes salary post-501(c)(3). The rebuttable-presumption procedure runs before the first paycheck. Founder may serve on Board through that period, then steps off Board no later than 24 months after taking salary.
- **Designated Acting ED.** Specific person to be named at the next annual board meeting once initial board is seated.
- **Outside-counsel retainer.** For routine governance questions. Worth setting up before the first hard transition rather than during it.
- **D&O tail coverage.** Typical 3–6 year tail. Confirm with NIA or whichever carrier is chosen.
- **Founder's continuing involvement scope.** Drafted abstractly; concrete agreement will be the founder's individual decision at the time of transition.

## Part IV. Narrative description of your activities

### Past, present, and planned activities

**HAND Protocol Foundation** (the "Organization") was incorporated as a Texas nonprofit corporation in 2026 to operate a curated resource pool through which skilled Contributors and three communities of regenerative impact work (practitioners who heal, founders who build, and organizers who steward, collectively "Reciprocates") match to meet mutual needs, with long-term branding, marketing, technology, design, and operational accompaniment provided to Reciprocates as the core service. The Organization operates primarily in Austin, Texas, and serves participants nationally through web-based engagement and selected in-person work.

The Organization's activities are organized into two integrated programs.

### Program 1: Reciprocate Accompaniment via Resource Pool

**Purpose.** To address a documented gap in the U.S. capacity-building ecosystem: small organizations and individual practitioners doing socially significant work who fall through the gaps of existing capacity-building infrastructure. They are too small for institutional capacity-building (such as Bridgespan), too pre-revenue for many foundation funding cycles, too relationally specific for project-bounded pro bono services (such as Taproot Foundation or Catchafire), and too operationally fragile to thrive on short-term volunteer help.

**Population served.** The Organization serves three communities of regenerative impact work, collectively referred to as **Reciprocates**:

a. **Healers and wellness practitioners.** Solo or small-team bodyworkers, somatic therapists, herbalists, indigenous and ancestral practitioners, harm-reduction-informed healers.

b. **Impact entrepreneurs.** Founders of mission-driven small ventures, typically post-accelerator, in social enterprise, B-corp, nonprofit pilot, or hybrid structures.

c. **Grassroots community organizations.** Mutual aid networks, harm reduction collectives, food sovereignty groups, land stewardship projects, often serving populations systematically failed by institutions.

The lines between these communities are porous; many Reciprocates live in two of these worlds at once (a herbalist running a community apothecary; a harm-reduction organizer whose work is also a healing practice; a regenerative-agriculture entrepreneur stewarding a co-op).

**The resource pool and the three flows.** The primary operating mechanism is a curated resource pool of volunteer skilled professionals and tradespeople ("**Contributors**"). Contributors and Reciprocates participate through three named flows:

a. **Donate.** A Contributor offers hours, deliverables, or ongoing service to the pool without expecting anything specific in return. Donations are tax-deductible under the Organization's exempt status. Example: a designer donates twelve hours per month to whichever Reciprocate the Organization matches them with.

b. **Exchange.** A Contributor requests a specific skill from the pool in return for their own contribution. The Organization matches both sides, sets clear scope on both engagements, and stays present if friction arises. Example: a roofer repairs community-garden sheds in exchange for a website and brand support.

c. **Receive.** A Reciprocate applies for support. The Organization vets fit, scope, and capacity, then matches from the pool, builds the work directly, or both, and stays for upkeep over the planned engagement (default term: three years). Example: a food-sovereignty collective receives a designer, a donation page, and twelve months of operations support; reciprocal contribution is transparency and storytelling that strengthens the pool.

The pool is designed so that everyone involved can benefit if they choose to. A single person can be both giver and receiver: the reiki practitioner who needs a website is also offering reiki to the pool; an impact founder who needs design help can mentor a younger founder in return. Cross-participation is encouraged but never required.

**Activities.** Within and through the resource pool, the Organization provides to Reciprocates, free of charge or at substantially below-market rates:

- Development of web presence: website design, deployment, training in maintenance.
- Brand articulation: voice, identity, plain-language positioning.
- Operational infrastructure: scheduling systems, financial tools, communication systems, document management.
- Sustained accompaniment: a dedicated program lead at the Organization maintains the relationship for a planned term (default: three years), with monthly working sessions, quarterly reflection, and annual planning.
- Curated matching: Contributors are screened and matched to specific Reciprocate needs based on skill fit, capacity, and mutual fit. Both parties consent before any introduction.
- A graduation protocol: at the end of the engagement, infrastructure is fully transferred to the Reciprocate's ownership and control, and the Reciprocate enters alumni status. Graduated Reciprocates may return to the pool as Contributors, mentors, or cross-cohort connectors. Graduation is entry into a broader pool, not exit from a program.

**Contributor recruitment, screening, and recognition.** Contributors are recruited through public channels and apply through a documented process including skills inventory, capacity statement, references, and Code of Ethics acknowledgment. References are checked for Contributors offering services with material trust implications. Accepted Contributors receive a handbook and ongoing recognition appropriate to their preferences. Contributors are volunteers, not employees; they receive no compensation except for documented expense reimbursement under an accountable plan, modest honoraria for intensive engagements (recorded with 1099-NEC if cumulative payments exceed $600), and de minimis recognition gifts.

**How the Organization selects Reciprocates.** A documented selection process (described in [`../programs/reciprocate-selection-and-graduation.md`](../programs/reciprocate-selection-and-graduation.md)): inbound interest, exploratory conversation, written application, second conversation, reference checks, internal review against published criteria, and decision communicated within 14 days.

**Charitable purpose.** This program furthers the Organization's exempt purposes by:

a. **Education** of Reciprocates in the operational, marketing, technological, and brand-development competencies they need to serve their populations sustainably; and education of Contributors in the practical work of accompaniment. The Organization develops curricula, documentation, and case studies as part of the program.

b. **Charitable activity** in lessening the burdens of small organizations and practitioners serving people experiencing poverty, illness, and other forms of distress. The Reciprocates in turn provide health, healing, food security, harm-reduction, and community-organizing services to the public.

c. **Charitable activity** in combating community deterioration by strengthening the operational infrastructure of grassroots community organizations through skilled-volunteer matching and mutual exchange.

The Organization does **not** provide direct services to the Reciprocates' clients or constituents. The Organization serves the Reciprocates, who in turn serve the public.

**Public benefit.** Reciprocates are selected on documented criteria including the public-good nature of their work, identifiable beneficiaries, and operational shape consistent with the Organization's program design. No Reciprocate or Contributor is selected based on personal relationships or for the private benefit of any director, officer, or key employee. The Organization's Conflict of Interest Policy governs all selection decisions.

### Program 2: Sovereign Reciprocates (optional AI augmentation)

**Purpose.** Many Reciprocates have administrative burdens that limit the time and attention they can devote to their work. For Reciprocate groups who choose it, the Organization builds custom open-source artificial intelligence agent systems that augment the human accompaniment without replacing it. Sovereign Reciprocates is one tool inside the resource pool, available to Reciprocate groups who opt in. The accompaniment work in Program 1 functions whether or not a given Reciprocate engages with Sovereign Reciprocates.

**Activities.**

a. **Per-group agent systems.** For each consenting Reciprocate group, the Organization builds a custom agent system: a small fine-tuned model on an open-source base (Llama 3.1 8B, Mistral 7B, or successor), with a per-group LoRA adapter trained on the group's own work, retrieval over the group's own document library, and tool access scoped to the group's workflows. A human-review gate sits before any external output.

b. **Open-source release.** The Organization publishes the methodology, agent scaffolding code, evaluation framework, and adapter training methodology under permissive licenses (MIT for code, CC BY-SA 4.0 for documentation).

c. **Quarterly and annual accountability.** Quarterly evaluation reports on the program's operations, evaluation outcomes, and any incidents. Annual sovereignty drills test adapter portability and consent revocation; closure simulations are conducted at major program decision points. The Annual AI Accountability Report is published yearly.

**Eight sovereignty principles.** The program operates under eight binding principles ([`../policies/data-sovereignty-and-ai.md`](../policies/data-sovereignty-and-ai.md)): open base model; Reciprocate-group-owned adapter; revocable training consent; no cross-Reciprocate-group data extraction; self-hostable end-to-end; full audit trail inspectable by the group; open methodology, code, evaluation, and quarterly reports; case-by-case adapter weights publication.

**Opt-in and group ownership.** No Reciprocate is required to engage with Sovereign Reciprocates as a condition of receiving accompaniment. Reciprocate groups who opt in retain ownership of the resulting adapters, training data, evaluation logs, and inference recipes. The Organization holds these artifacts in trust and transfers them to the Reciprocate group on departure, request, or dissolution. The Organization's Bylaws (Article XII) and the Data Sovereignty and AI Policy bind the Organization to these commitments; the Bylaws further require Reciprocate-group adapter handoff before any general asset distribution on dissolution.

**Charitable purpose.** This program furthers the Organization's exempt purposes by:

a. **Scientific activity.** Conducting and publishing research on responsible AI development for small grassroots organizations.

b. **Educational activity.** The methodology, code, evaluation framework, and quarterly reports are educational resources for the field.

c. **Charitable activity** in providing operational infrastructure to opted-in Reciprocate groups that lessens the burden on small organizations serving people experiencing poverty, illness, and other forms of distress.

**No private benefit.** The Organization does not retain proprietary rights in the per-group adapters; those are owned by the respective Reciprocate groups. The Organization does not sell access, monetize, or commercialize the systems. The Organization does not aggregate Reciprocate-group data into any "platform model." Directors, officers, and key employees do not personally profit from the program.

### Cross-program activities

In addition to the two programs, the Organization conducts the following activities supporting both:

**Research and publication.** The Organization conducts research on capacity-building, accompaniment models, and responsible AI infrastructure, publishing the results openly. Three discovery research documents are currently published at handprotocol.org/discovery. The Organization expects to publish a methodology paper on the Sovereign Reciprocates program once the pilot cohort completes.

**Education and convening.** The Organization participates in field convenings, sometimes as host, sometimes as participant. The Organization makes its methodology and case studies available to adjacent organizations and field-building events.

**Governance and transparency.** The Organization publishes its governance documents (Bylaws, Articles, all policies, theory of change, strategic plan, compliance calendar) on its public website, exceeding the legal minimum.

## Part V. Compensation and other financial arrangements

**Compensation of officers and directors.** Directors serve without compensation for board service. Officers serve without compensation for officer service. The Organization's Bylaws (§4.8) prohibit compensation for board or officer service except for reimbursement of documented expenses.

**Compensation of employees.** The Organization currently has no paid employees. The founder operates as an unpaid Executive Director through the filing period. When the Organization hires its first paid employee (likely Executive Director or Program Director), compensation will be set under the Organization's Executive Compensation Policy ([`../policies/executive-compensation.md`](../policies/executive-compensation.md)), which implements the rebuttable-presumption procedure under Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6.

**No loans.** The Organization does not make loans to directors, officers, employees, or related parties (Bylaws §9.4).

**No private benefit.** The Organization's activities do not provide private benefit to any private individual or organization beyond what is incidental to the charitable activities. The Reciprocates and Contributors receive benefits as members of charitable classes (described in Part IV), not as identified individuals chosen for personal reasons.

**Conflict of Interest Policy.** Adopted concurrent with the Bylaws. Each director, officer, and key employee signs the policy and an annual disclosure form. The policy follows the IRS sample in Appendix A of the Form 1023 instructions, with HAND-specific additions covering AI workstream conflicts.

## Part VI. Members and other individuals and organizations that receive benefits

**Members.** The Organization has no voting members (Bylaws Article III). The Board of Directors manages the affairs of the Organization.

**Persons benefiting from the Organization's activities.** Reciprocates (individuals and small organizations as described in Part IV) and, indirectly, the populations the Reciprocates serve.

**Selection of beneficiaries.**

a. **Reciprocates are selected** through the documented Reciprocate Selection process, based on objective criteria including mission fit, operational shape, relational readiness, and capacity to engage in a multi-year relationship. Selection does not turn on personal relationships with directors, officers, or key employees.

b. **Contributors are selected** through the documented Contributor Application process, based on skills, capacity, mission alignment, and reference checks.

c. **The Sovereign Reciprocates program** operates only with Reciprocate groups already selected through Program 1, and only with the Reciprocate group's explicit Reciprocate Consent Framework signed.

**Charitable class.** The Organization serves a charitable class of small organizations and individual practitioners doing socially significant work who lack access to existing capacity-building infrastructure. The class is large and indefinite; participants are not pre-selected individuals.

## Part VIII. Specific activities

### Are you a successor to another organization?

The Organization is not a successor to another nonprofit. However, the Organization's name and certain pre-incorporation work originated under an unincorporated project active from August 2024 to early 2026, focused initially on Web3-based fundraising infrastructure. The pre-incorporation project did not have separate legal existence or tax-exempt status. In early 2026, the project pivoted away from Web3-first framing toward the current Reciprocate-accompaniment model and is being formally incorporated under that revised mission. The pre-incorporation activities, assets, and intellectual property are being transferred to the Organization on incorporation.

### Do you support or oppose candidates for public office?

No. The Organization does not engage in any political campaign activity (Articles of Incorporation Article 4; Bylaws Article 2).

### Do you attempt to influence legislation?

No substantial part of the Organization's activities consists of attempting to influence legislation. The Organization may, from time to time, communicate publicly on policy issues affecting capacity-building, grassroots organizations, or responsible AI, but does not engage in lobbying as defined under IRC §501(c)(3) and §4911.

### Do you operate bingo or other games?

No.

### Do you conduct fundraising activities?

Yes. The Organization conducts fundraising through:

a. **Individual contributions** solicited through the website, email, and direct outreach.

b. **Foundation grants** through letters of inquiry and full applications.

c. **Cryptocurrency contributions** accepted under the Gift Acceptance Policy through a dedicated multi-signature wallet, converted to USD within 30 days of receipt by default.

d. **In-kind contributions** of professional services from Contributors and others.

The Organization's Gift Acceptance Policy ([`../policies/gift-acceptance.md`](../policies/gift-acceptance.md)) governs all fundraising activities, including donor acknowledgment, restrictions, and recognition. The Organization is based in Texas, which does not require general charitable solicitation registration. The Organization solicits primarily through web-based passive presence and has no immediate plans to register in additional states; multi-state solicitation registration will be considered if and when triggers under the Charleston Principles are met.

### Do you have written or oral agreements with related parties?

No agreements with related parties as defined in Form 1023 instructions at this time.

### Do you provide goods or services to any government agency?

No.

### Do you have a close connection with any organizations?

The Organization may, during the period before receipt of the IRS Determination Letter, operate under a fiscal sponsorship arrangement with a qualifying 501(c)(3) public charity. If so, the relationship will be a Model C ("Pre-approved Grant Relationship") arrangement under which the sponsor receives tax-deductible contributions and re-grants them to the Organization. Sponsor candidates include Players Philanthropy Fund and Hack Club Bank. The Organization will exit the sponsorship within 60 days of receiving the IRS Determination Letter. Fiscal sponsorship is not a related-party arrangement in the traditional sense; it is a service arrangement between independent organizations.

### Are you applying for retroactive recognition?

The Organization is filing Form 1023 within 27 months of its date of formation, requesting that the effective date of exempt status be the date of formation pursuant to Treas. Reg. §1.508-1(a)(2)(i).

## Part X. Public charity status

The Organization requests classification as a **public charity** under **IRC §509(a)(1)** and **§170(b)(1)(A)(vi)**, the broadly-supported public charity category.

### Basis for classification

The Organization will receive support from a broad base of public sources, including:

a. **Individual contributors** at angel-number tier levels ($222, $2,222, $22,777, $77,444, $222,222 and intermediate amounts). The Organization's fundraising design diversifies donor base across many small to medium contributors rather than concentrating in a few large donors.

b. **Foundation grants** from multiple foundations expected over a five-year horizon, including community foundations (Hogg Foundation, St. David's Foundation, Austin Community Foundation), national mission-aligned funders (Kataly, Solidaire), and AI-aligned funders (Patrick J. McGovern Foundation, Mozilla Foundation).

c. **Workplace giving and donor-advised fund grants** through standard platforms.

The Organization's strategic plan ([`../programs/strategic-plan-2026-2028.md`](../programs/strategic-plan-2026-2028.md)) targets revenue diversification such that no single funder represents more than 40% of revenue by Year 3.

### Public support test compliance

The Organization will monitor compliance with the §170(b)(1)(A)(vi) public support test on a continuous basis. The Treasurer is responsible for the calculation, with the 2% per-donor cap applied to individual donor contributions. The Organization's Gift Acceptance Policy provides for review of any individual gift that would exceed 2% of expected 5-year revenue, with restructuring options including multi-year structure or matching solicitation.

The Organization is exempt from the public support test calculation for its first 5 years. Beginning Year 6, the Organization will calculate the test annually on a rolling 5-year basis as required by the Pension Protection Act amendments.

## Drafting notes and open questions

These notes are for HAND's counsel-review process and are **not** included in the actual Form 1023 submission.

### Drafting choices made

a. **Filed long-form Form 1023, not 1023-EZ.** Because: (i) projected receipts will likely exceed $50K once filing-raise closes; (ii) the narrative is what funders read; (iii) the long form preserves full retroactive-date flexibility; (iv) the long form forces drafting the policies funders look for.

b. **Public charity under §509(a)(1) / §170(b)(1)(A)(vi),** not §509(a)(2). Because HAND's revenue mix is donations and grants, not exempt-function gross receipts. The broadly-supported test is the right fit.

c. **Retroactive effective date.** Filed within 27 months of formation. Schedule E not needed.

d. **Sovereign Reciprocates described as program 3,** integrated into the activity narrative rather than buried. Because: AI funders read this language directly; sovereignty principles are mission-defining, not implementation detail.

e. **Reciprocates and Contributors capitalized as defined terms.** Establishes the vocabulary the Organization uses operationally.

### Open questions for counsel

- **Cryptocurrency disclosure depth.** Drafted briefly. Counsel may want more detail per IRS Chief Counsel Memo on crypto donations.
- **Fiscal sponsor disclosure if applicable.** Drafted assuming sponsorship may apply. Adjust based on actual decision before filing.
- **Pre-incorporation activity attribution.** Drafted to acknowledge the August 2024 pre-incorporation project and the early-2026 pivot. Counsel may want to refine the "successor" framing.
- **Specific activity descriptions.** May need more detail if the IRS examiner is unfamiliar with sovereignty-aware AI work. Plain-language explanation should bridge the gap.
- **Multi-state solicitation.** Drafted as currently not registered anywhere; not required. Counsel should confirm.
- **Schedule attachments.** Counsel should review the complete list and structure before filing.

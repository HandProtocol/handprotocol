# Develop Pillar — Contributor Onboarding

> How you join the Develop pillar, how we build the work around your life instead of asking your life to bend around the work, and what a delivery sprint actually feels like from the inside.

**Status:** Draft v0.1 · June 2026 · Pre-adoption
**Audience:** Incoming HAND workers and Contributors doing Develop-pillar delivery (web / SEO / e-commerce builds for local businesses)
**Companion docs:** [Volunteer and Contributor Policy](../policies/volunteer-and-contributor.md) (the legal frame) · [biz-outreach PRD §10.5](../../funding/grants/_hand-biz-outreach-prd.md) (the delivery model) · [Reciprocate Selection and Graduation](reciprocate-selection-and-graduation.md)

## 1. What the Develop pillar is, in one breath

HAND closes web/SEO/e-commerce work for local businesses, and a curated team of AI agents does the heavy lifting under a HAND worker's supervision. A minimum of 33% of every dollar routes into HAND's resource pool; the rest pays the people who do the work and keeps the lights on. Your job is not to be the agency. Your job is to **know the customer, point the agents at the right outcome, and make sure what ships is good.**

If that sounds like a lot, read the next section first. It's the most important one.

## 2. The promise: we work around your schedule

This is the part most onboarding documents bury. We're putting it second, right after the one-line "what is this," because it's the thing that actually determines whether this works for you.

**You tell us your capacity. We build the work to fit it — not the other way around.**

Concretely, that means:

- **You set the number.** Two hours a week, ten hours a week, a burst of thirty hours one month and then nothing the next — all of these are real, supported answers. You give us a capacity statement when you join (hours per month, and how far ahead you can commit), and you update it whenever life changes. ([Volunteer Policy §3c, §6c](../policies/volunteer-and-contributor.md).)
- **There are no fixed hours and no standing meetings you must attend.** The work is async-first. You pick up your part of a project on your own clock — early mornings, late nights, weekends, three days on and four days off. What matters is that you hit the review points you agreed to in the sprint, not that you were online at 2pm on a Tuesday.
- **You opt into work; it is never assigned to you cold.** You see what's in the pipeline and you raise your hand for what fits your skills and your current bandwidth. Nothing lands on your plate without you saying yes.
- **You can pause, and you can leave, without penalty.** If your capacity drops to zero for a season, you say so and we transition the work. Your standing in the pool doesn't suffer. When you're ready, you come back. ([Volunteer Policy §6c, §12a](../policies/volunteer-and-contributor.md).)
- **The three-project cap is a floor for your wellbeing, not a ceiling on your ambition.** No worker leads more than three concurrent projects — full stop. Review work is mentally heavier than people expect, and we'd rather you do three projects well and sustainably than five projects on the way to burning out. ([PRD §10.5](../../funding/grants/_hand-biz-outreach-prd.md).)

The pool model is the whole reason this can be flexible. You're not an employee on a timesheet. You contribute skill when you have it, you draw resources from the pool when you need them, and the math works because the agents absorb the grind that would otherwise demand a 40-hour week from a human.

> **The short version:** life first, work second. We designed the pillar so that being a parent, having a day job, having a hard month, or simply wanting your evenings back are all compatible with contributing here.

## 3. How onboarding actually goes

Lightweight on purpose. Most people are doing their first review within a week or two of saying yes.

1. **Application + capacity statement.** Skills, areas of interest, and — the important one — how much time you realistically have and how far out you can plan. Honest small numbers beat optimistic big ones. ([Volunteer Policy §3](../policies/volunteer-and-contributor.md).)
2. **Orientation conversation.** One real conversation with HAND, not a slide deck. We walk the pillar, the pool, the sovereignty principles, and — mostly — listen to how you want to work.
3. **Handbook + access.** You get the Contributor Handbook, sign the Code of Ethics acknowledgment, and receive access to the Command Center and the comms channels. ([Volunteer Policy §4](../policies/volunteer-and-contributor.md).)
4. **Shadow before solo.** Your first sprint, you ride along on someone else's project as a second reviewer. Low stakes, real context. You see the rhythm before you carry it.
5. **Your first solo sprint** — on a Starter-tier build (the smallest), scoped to a few hours total, with a more experienced worker one message away.

You are never thrown into a Premium build on day one. The work scales up only as fast as you want it to.

## 4. What a sprint is, and what to expect in one

A **sprint** is a time-boxed burst of delivery work that takes one customer project from kickoff to shipped. It is the basic unit of doing on the Develop pillar. You don't sign up for "a job"; you sign up for a sprint, you finish it, and then you decide — based on your life that week — whether to pick up the next one.

### 4.1 How sprints fit around your schedule

This is where the flexibility from §2 becomes concrete:

- **You opt into a sprint when you have the capacity for it.** Between sprints there is no obligation. A sprint is a container with a clear start and a clear end, which is exactly what makes it possible to do this work around a real life — you can see the whole commitment before you accept it.
- **Sprint size is matched to your stated capacity.** A Starter sprint is a few hours total. A Standard sprint is eight to twelve hours of *your* time spread across roughly a week or two. A Premium sprint is heavier and longer — and we only offer those to workers who've told us they have the room. ([PRD §10.5 team table](../../funding/grants/_hand-biz-outreach-prd.md).)
- **The hours are yours to place.** A "one-week sprint" does not mean nine-to-five for a week. It means a handful of review points across that week that you schedule when it suits you.

| Tier | Your time in the sprint | Typical wall-clock length |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | 2–4 hours total | A few days |
| Standard | 8–12 hours total | ~1–2 weeks |
| Premium | 20–30 hours over the first three months, then 5–8 hrs/month | Phased, ongoing |
| Custom | Scoped with you before you accept | Scoped per project |

### 4.2 The shape of a sprint

Every sprint, regardless of tier, moves through the same loop. Your role is the reviewer/integrator at each checkpoint — not the one producing the raw work.

1. **Kickoff.** You get the project brief: who the customer is, what they sell, what they need, the per-tier quality rubric. You spend your first chunk of time getting to *know the customer*, because that knowledge is the thing only a human can hold.
2. **Agents draft.** The agent team (design, content, build, and others depending on tier) produces the first pass — mockups, copy, code. This happens largely without you; you're not babysitting the keystrokes.
3. **You review and steer.** You check each draft against the brief and the rubric. For each piece you do one of three things: **ship it** with your sign-off, **send it back** to the agent with specific feedback, or **escalate** to a second worker for a fresh set of eyes. This is the heart of the job. ([PRD §10.5 QA loop](../../funding/grants/_hand-biz-outreach-prd.md).)
4. **Customer feedback flows through you.** The customer talks to you, not to the agents. You are the trust layer; the customer hears your name and sees your face, and you translate their feedback back into agent direction.
5. **Ship.** Nothing reaches the customer without a HAND worker's sign-off. When it's good, it goes live with your name on the relationship.
6. **Retrospective.** At project close we capture what the agents did well, what needed redoing, and what to add to the open playbook. Five minutes that make every future sprint easier.

### 4.3 What a working session inside a sprint feels like

You sit down for your hour. The agents have left you a stack of drafts in the Command Center. You read the customer brief again to keep their voice in your head. You go through the drafts: this hero section is right, ship it; this copy block sounds like a robot, send it back with "make it sound like the owner is talking to a neighbor"; this layout needs a second opinion, flag it. You draft a short status note to the customer (the comms agent has a first version waiting; you make it sound like you). You log off. The agents pick up your feedback and re-draft while you're gone. Next session, you check their second pass.

That's the texture: **review, steer, sign off, repeat — on your clock, in bursts you choose.**

### 4.4 What sprints are *not*

- **Not a crunch.** A sprint is time-boxed precisely so it *ends*. We are not asking you to live inside a permanent deadline.
- **Not you doing the agent's job.** If you find yourself writing the code or the copy from scratch, something has gone wrong — escalate, don't grind.
- **Not solo when it shouldn't be.** Standard and Premium sprints have a second worker available by design. Asking for backup is the system working, not you failing.
- **Not a commitment to the next one.** Each sprint stands alone. Finishing one obligates you to nothing.

## 5. What we ask of you in return

In exchange for building the work around your life, we ask for a few things that keep the whole thing trustworthy ([Volunteer Policy §8](../policies/volunteer-and-contributor.md)):

- **Honor the capacity you stated.** Show up to the review points you agreed to, and tell us early — not late — when something changes. The flexibility runs on honest communication.
- **Hold the quality bar.** Your sign-off means "this is genuinely good." It's the promise the customer is paying for.
- **Keep the customer at the center.** Their outcome and their voice over your preferred approach.
- **Stay aligned with the sovereignty principles.** Open methodology, group-owned configuration, the open option always live. Every delivery team is a Sovereign Reciprocates instance in revenue mode. ([PRD §10.5](../../funding/grants/_hand-biz-outreach-prd.md).)
- **Give honest retros.** What worked, what didn't. The playbook only gets better if you're candid.

## 6. Quick reference

| You're wondering… | The answer |
|---|---|
| How many hours must I commit? | Whatever you state. Two a week is welcome. |
| Are there mandatory meetings? | No standing ones. Work is async; one orientation conversation up front. |
| What if I get slammed by life? | Tell us, pause, hand off. No penalty, your pool standing is safe. |
| How many projects will I juggle? | Three concurrent, maximum — that's a hard cap for your sake. |
| Do I write the websites myself? | No. Agents draft; you review, steer, and sign off. |
| What does a sprint commit me to? | Just that one sprint. The next is always a fresh yes. |
| Am I paid? | Reciprocated internal rates ($40–80/hr), plus the pool-draw model. See [Volunteer Policy §7, §9](../policies/volunteer-and-contributor.md). |
| What if a draft isn't good enough? | Send it back, or escalate to a second worker. Both are normal. |

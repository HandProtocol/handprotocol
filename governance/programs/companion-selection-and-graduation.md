# Companion Selection and Graduation Protocol

> How HAND Protocol Foundation chooses Companions, what HAND commits to during the engagement, and how engagements end with sovereignty handoff.

**Status:** Draft v0.1 · May 2026 · Living document

---

## Purpose

This document covers the lifecycle of HAND's relationship with Companions: how Companions are identified and selected, what HAND commits to during the engagement, what is expected of the Companion, and how the engagement ends, whether by graduation, transition, or withdrawal.

The protocol reflects HAND's defining commitment: long-term relational accompaniment, not project-bounded engagement.

## Section 1. Companion definition

A **Companion** is an individual practitioner or small organization HAND accompanies. HAND serves three communities of regenerative impact work: **practitioners who heal, founders who build, and organizers who steward**. The lines between them are porous; many Companions live in two of these worlds at once.

a. **Healers and wellness practitioners.** Solo or small-team. Bodyworkers, somatic practitioners, reiki and energy work, herbalists, indigenous and ancestral practice, harm-reduction-informed healing.

b. **Impact entrepreneurs.** Mission-driven small ventures, often post-accelerator. B-corps, social enterprises, mission-aligned LLCs, hybrid for-profit/nonprofit structures.

c. **Grassroots community organizations.** Mutual aid networks, harm reduction collectives, food sovereignty groups, land stewardship projects, often without formal 501(c)(3) status.

The three segments share an operational shape: small, under-resourced for infrastructure, serving people who need them, vulnerable to abandonment by traditional capacity-building. The categories are illustrative, not exhaustive. A herbalist running a community apothecary lives across (a) and (c); a harm-reduction organizer whose work is also a healing practice lives across (a) and (c); a regenerative-agriculture entrepreneur stewarding a co-op lives across (b) and (c). If the work is in this spirit, it likely fits.

## Section 2. Selection criteria

A Companion candidate is evaluated against these criteria. Not all must be met; the criteria are inputs to judgment, not a scorecard.

### Mission fit

a. The Companion's work serves the public good in a way consistent with HAND's mission.

b. The Companion's beneficiaries / clients / community are real and identifiable.

c. The Companion's stated purpose and operational behavior are aligned (no greenwashing, mission drift, or vanity projects).

### Operational shape

a. The Companion is small enough that HAND's accompaniment is meaningful (a 500-person organization does not need HAND).

b. The Companion has a sufficient operational track record to be assessed (not pure idea-stage).

c. The Companion needs the kind of infrastructure HAND provides (web, brand, operations, possibly agent systems).

d. The Companion does not have the in-house capacity or budget to build the infrastructure themselves.

### Relational fit

a. The Companion seeks a multi-year relationship, not a one-time project.

b. The Companion is willing to engage HAND staff and Contributors in regular check-ins and shared planning.

c. The Companion is reachable and responsive.

d. The Companion's principals are emotionally and practically present for the work (this matters in healer and grassroots segments especially, where founder availability shapes everything).

### Risk and capacity

a. HAND has the capacity to take on a new Companion at the time.

b. The Companion's work does not raise legal, reputational, or safety risks HAND cannot manage.

c. The Companion's work does not pose conflicts with existing Companions or Contributors.

### Companion-side commitment

a. The Companion understands HAND is not a money-distributing organization.

b. The Companion understands the relationship is HAND-mediated; staff turnover is anticipated.

c. The Companion understands that infrastructure remains with them at graduation, with Sovereign Companions adapters belonging to them outright.

### Equity considerations

a. HAND specifically welcomes Companions from communities systematically underserved by traditional capacity-building infrastructure: BIPOC-led, queer-led, disability-led, indigenous-led, working-class-led.

b. Lived-experience leadership (people leading organizations that serve their own communities) is a positive selection factor.

c. HAND does not require any specific demographic profile; selection is about fit with mission and operational shape, not quotas.

## Section 3. Selection process

Companion selection follows a documented process:

a. **Inbound interest.** Companions discover HAND through the website, peer referrals, foundation introductions, or HAND's outreach.

b. **First conversation.** A 30-minute exploratory conversation with HAND staff. Both sides share what they're looking for. No commitments.

c. **Application.** A short written application: who you are, what you do, what you need, what you can give back, why HAND.

d. **Second conversation.** Deeper conversation, 60-90 minutes, exploring mutual fit. Includes another HAND staff member or Board member.

e. **Reference and verification.** HAND speaks to 2-3 references (Companion's peers, prior collaborators, beneficiaries with the Companion's permission).

f. **HAND internal review.** Staff and Companion program lead review the application against the criteria in Section 2. Conflicts of interest disclosed.

g. **Decision.** Acceptance, declination, or "let's stay in touch" (for Companions who are a strong fit but with timing or capacity constraints).

h. **Communication.** Decision communicated within 14 days of the second conversation, with reasoning.

i. **Onboarding.** Accepted Companions sign an engagement agreement (Section 4) and begin the engagement.

The process typically takes 4-8 weeks from inbound interest to acceptance.

## Section 4. Engagement agreement

When a Companion is accepted, a written Engagement Agreement documents:

a. **Names and parties.** HAND Protocol Foundation; the Companion (and signatory if organizational).

b. **Term.** Default three-year initial term, renewable. Either party may withdraw on notice per Section 9.

c. **Scope of work.** What HAND will provide (web, brand, operational tools, Sovereign Companions option, accompaniment cadence). Detailed in a separate Scope Annex updated annually.

d. **Cadence of contact.** Default: monthly working sessions, quarterly reflection, annual planning. Adjustable to the Companion's actual pace.

e. **Mutual commitments.** What HAND commits to. What the Companion commits to (responsiveness, engagement with Contributors, honest feedback).

f. **Confidentiality.** Information shared by the Companion is held in confidence per Privacy Policy.

g. **Sovereignty.** Where Sovereign Companions is part of the engagement, the Companion Consent Framework ([`../policies/data-sovereignty-and-ai.md`](../policies/data-sovereignty-and-ai.md) §4) is attached.

h. **Recognition.** How HAND may speak publicly about the engagement (default: no public mention without case-by-case consent).

i. **Conflict resolution.** Disagreements addressed first directly, then through HAND's program lead, then through the Executive Director, then through Board mediation if necessary.

j. **Termination.** Either party may end the engagement on 60 days' written notice. Transitions handled per Section 9.

k. **Cost.** Default: free to the Companion. HAND may negotiate cost-sharing in specific cases (e.g., for Companions with budget capacity who want premium support); decisions are case-by-case and not the default.

Both parties sign and retain.

## Section 5. During engagement

a. **Program lead assigned.** Each Companion has a designated program lead at HAND.

b. **Working rhythm.** Established in the first 30 days. Typically monthly sessions plus async communication.

c. **Quarterly reflection.** Joint review of what's working, what isn't, what to adjust.

d. **Annual planning.** What the next year looks like. Update Scope Annex.

e. **Contributor matches.** As Contributor needs arise, HAND-mediated matching per [`../policies/volunteer-and-contributor.md`](../policies/volunteer-and-contributor.md).

f. **Sovereign Companions deployment.** Optional. If pursued, Companion Consent Framework signed first; deployment follows the AI policy.

g. **Documentation.** HAND maintains an engagement file with session notes, deliverables, feedback, and Companion communications. Companion has access to their own file.

h. **Conflicts of interest.** Either party may raise. Resolution per the Engagement Agreement.

## Section 6. Companion graduation

Graduation is the planned, healthy end of a Companion engagement. It is **not** failure; it is the intended outcome.

### When graduation is appropriate

a. The Companion has the infrastructure they need.

b. The Companion can independently maintain the infrastructure (with optional alumni-tier support from HAND).

c. The relationship has been mutually rewarding and is reaching its natural arc.

d. The Companion expresses readiness or HAND raises the conversation.

Graduation typically occurs at the end of Year 3 of the engagement, but may be earlier if mutual readiness exists.

### Graduation process

a. **Conversation.** Companion and HAND staff discuss graduation 6 months in advance.

b. **Documented transition plan.** What does the Companion need from HAND in the final 6 months to be fully independent? What stays with the Companion? Who is HAND's alumni contact?

c. **Sovereignty handoff (if applicable).** Adapter weights, training data, evaluation logs, inference recipe handed over with documentation. Companion signs receipt confirming the handoff. Sovereignty principle 2 operationalized.

d. **Final session.** A formal close: review of the engagement, lessons learned, Companion's reflections, HAND's reflections.

e. **Alumni status.** The Companion enters alumni status with HAND. Optional ongoing contact: quarterly check-ins for two years, then annual; access to HAND community spaces; option to return for renewed engagement if circumstances change.

f. **Documentation.** Engagement file finalized and archived per Document Retention Policy.

g. **Public acknowledgment.** With Companion consent, HAND may publish a case study, mention the engagement in the Annual Report, or invite the Companion to speak at a HAND convening.

### Alumni pathway

Graduation is entry into a broader pool, not exit from a program. Graduated Companions remain part of HAND's resource pool with full optionality:

a. **Alumni Companions remain part of HAND's community** indefinitely. Optional quarterly check-ins for two years post-graduation, then annual; access to community spaces; option to return for renewed engagement if circumstances change.

b. **Alumni may return as Contributors.** The same flywheel that supported them is now one they can feed. Returning Contributors apply per the standard Contributor process but enter with the relational trust of prior engagement; reference checks may be lighter where the Corporation already has direct working knowledge.

c. **Alumni may mentor incoming Companions.** Cross-cohort connections are explicitly welcomed: a graduated Companion can pair with an incoming Companion in similar work to share what they learned, what surprised them, and what the engagement looked like up close. Mentorship pairings are voluntary on both sides and structured with light scaffolding by HAND staff.

d. **Alumni may serve as cross-cohort connectors** for HAND staff: helping with intake conversations, evaluation discussions, or program design where their lived experience is relevant. Stipends or honoraria may apply for substantial connector work.

e. **Alumni have unrestricted alumni-tier access** to publicly available HAND resources (open-source code, methodology, evaluation reports, the Annual Flywheel Health Check, the Annual AI Accountability Report).

f. **Sovereignty commitments do not end at graduation.** HAND continues to honor the eight Sovereign Companions principles for the graduated Companion's adapter for as long as it exists, including continued portability and revocation rights.

g. **No reciprocity quota.** Alumni participation is voluntary in every direction. A graduated Companion who chooses no continuing engagement is fully welcomed; an alumni Contributor who later needs to Receive again is fully welcomed.

The alumni pathway is the resource pool's primary mechanism of self-reinforcement: each engagement, in time, contributes back to the pool that supported it.

## Section 7. Mid-engagement transition

Sometimes a Companion is no longer the right fit before three years. Transitions handled with respect.

### When mid-engagement transition is appropriate

a. **Companion's needs have evolved** beyond what HAND provides.

b. **HAND's capacity has shifted** and the engagement cannot be sustained.

c. **Companion's circumstances have changed** (dissolution, merger, founder departure).

d. **Mutual fit has weakened** despite good-faith effort.

### Transition process

a. **Conversation.** Either party raises the question of transition.

b. **Joint deliberation.** Is this a temporary capacity issue resolvable by pausing? Or is this a real fit shift?

c. **Decision.** Continue, pause, or transition.

d. **Transition plan.** If transitioning, the next 60-90 days include completing in-flight work, handing off infrastructure, and sovereignty handoff.

e. **Alumni status.** Mid-engagement transitions still enter alumni status, with the same protections and offerings as graduation.

f. **Documentation.** Transition documented in the engagement file.

## Section 8. Companion withdrawal

A Companion may withdraw from the engagement at any time. HAND honors withdrawal without prejudice.

a. **Notice.** 60 days' written notice preferred; immediate withdrawal accepted if needed.

b. **Wind-down.** HAND completes in-flight commitments (a website near launch is finished; an active Contributor engagement is concluded).

c. **Sovereignty handoff.** Same as graduation if Sovereign Companions has been deployed.

d. **No penalty.** No financial obligation, no contractual penalty. The relationship simply ends.

e. **Alumni option.** Withdrawing Companions are welcome in alumni status.

## Section 9. HAND-initiated termination

In rare cases, HAND may terminate an engagement for cause.

### Grounds for HAND-initiated termination

a. **Conduct violating the Code of Ethics** (harassment, discrimination, financial impropriety, harm to staff or Contributors).

b. **Material misrepresentation** of the Companion's work or status.

c. **Sustained unresponsiveness** despite repeated attempts.

d. **Mission incompatibility** discovered after engagement begins.

e. **Legal or safety risk** to HAND, staff, or other Companions.

### Procedure

a. **Concern raised** through HAND's internal channels.

b. **Conversation with Companion** documenting the concern and seeking response.

c. **Decision** by Executive Director (and Board Chair for material cases).

d. **Communication** to Companion with written explanation.

e. **Transition or immediate close** per circumstances.

f. **Sovereignty handoff** is provided even in for-cause termination, **unless** withholding is required by court order or to prevent ongoing harm. HAND's sovereignty principles outlast disagreement.

g. **Documentation** retained per the Document Retention Policy.

## Section 10. Documentation and privacy

a. Engagement files are confidential.

b. Public communications about Companions require explicit Companion consent.

c. Aggregated, de-identified outcome data may be reported publicly; individual Companion identification only with consent.

d. Companion personal information protected per Privacy Policy.

## Section 11. Open questions

- **Cohort vs continuous selection.** Cohort 1 onboards roughly together; later years may shift to continuous selection. Operational complexity differs. Decide before Cohort 2.
- **Companion graduation timing.** Three years is the default; some Companions may benefit from longer or shorter. Build flexibility into the Engagement Agreement.
- **Cost-sharing for capacity Companions.** When a Companion has budget and could pay, should they? Drafted as case-by-case; consider whether to make this more explicit policy.
- **Alumni community structure.** Active design of the alumni community is a Year 2-3 project.
- **Companion-to-Contributor pathway.** Some Companion principals may want to become Contributors after graduation. Welcome; document explicitly.
- **Cross-Companion mentorship.** Could be valuable; needs structure to prevent overburdening busy practitioners. Pilot in Year 2.

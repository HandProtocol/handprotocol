# Reciprocate Selection and Graduation Protocol

> How HAND Protocol Foundation chooses Reciprocates, what HAND commits to during the engagement, and how engagements end with sovereignty handoff.

**Status:** Draft v0.1 · May 2026 · Living document

---

## Purpose

This document covers the lifecycle of HAND's relationship with Reciprocates: how Reciprocates are identified and selected, what HAND commits to during the engagement, what is expected of the Reciprocate, and how the engagement ends, whether by graduation, transition, or withdrawal.

The protocol reflects HAND's defining commitment: long-term relational accompaniment, not project-bounded engagement.

## Section 1. Reciprocate definition

A **Reciprocate** is an individual founder, small business, or small organization HAND accompanies. HAND serves three connected communities: **impact entrepreneurs, community-rooted small businesses, and grassroots organizations**. The lines between them are porous; many Reciprocates live in two of these worlds at once.

a. **Community-rooted small businesses.** Owner-operated or small-team restaurants, food producers, makers, trades, independent shops, and neighborhood services that create local jobs and meet everyday community needs.

b. **Impact entrepreneurs.** Mission-driven small ventures, often post-accelerator. B-corps, social enterprises, mission-aligned LLCs, hybrid for-profit/nonprofit structures.

c. **Grassroots community organizations.** Mutual aid networks, harm reduction collectives, food sovereignty groups, land stewardship projects, often without formal 501(c)(3) status.

The three segments share an operational shape: small, under-resourced for infrastructure, serving people who need them, vulnerable to abandonment by traditional capacity-building. The categories are illustrative, not exhaustive. A food entrepreneur building a neighborhood market lives across (a) and (b); a worker-owned service business lives across (a) and (c); a regenerative-agriculture entrepreneur stewarding a co-op lives across (b) and (c). If the work creates real community value, it likely fits.

## Section 2. Selection criteria

A Reciprocate candidate is evaluated against these criteria. Not all must be met; the criteria are inputs to judgment, not a scorecard.

### Mission fit

a. The Reciprocate's work serves the public good in a way consistent with HAND's mission.

b. The Reciprocate's beneficiaries / clients / community are real and identifiable.

c. The Reciprocate's stated purpose and operational behavior are aligned (no greenwashing, mission drift, or vanity projects).

### Operational shape

a. The Reciprocate is small enough that HAND's accompaniment is meaningful (a 500-person organization does not need HAND).

b. The Reciprocate has a sufficient operational track record to be assessed (not pure idea-stage).

c. The Reciprocate needs the kind of infrastructure HAND provides (web, brand, operations, possibly agent systems).

d. The Reciprocate does not have the in-house capacity or budget to build the infrastructure themselves.

### Relational fit

a. The Reciprocate seeks a multi-year relationship, not a one-time project.

b. The Reciprocate is willing to engage HAND staff and Contributors in regular check-ins and shared planning.

c. The Reciprocate is reachable and responsive.

d. The Reciprocate's principals are emotionally and practically present for the work (this matters in owner-operated business and grassroots segments especially, where founder availability shapes everything).

### Risk and capacity

a. HAND has the capacity to take on a new Reciprocate at the time.

b. The Reciprocate's work does not raise legal, reputational, or safety risks HAND cannot manage.

c. The Reciprocate's work does not pose conflicts with existing Reciprocates or Contributors.

### Reciprocate-side commitment

a. The Reciprocate understands HAND is not a money-distributing organization.

b. The Reciprocate understands the relationship is HAND-mediated; staff turnover is anticipated.

c. The Reciprocate understands that infrastructure remains with them at graduation, with Sovereign Reciprocates adapters belonging to them outright.

### Equity considerations

a. HAND specifically welcomes Reciprocates from communities systematically underserved by traditional capacity-building infrastructure: BIPOC-led, queer-led, disability-led, indigenous-led, working-class-led.

b. Lived-experience leadership (people leading organizations that serve their own communities) is a positive selection factor.

c. HAND does not require any specific demographic profile; selection is about fit with mission and operational shape, not quotas.

## Section 3. Selection process

Reciprocate selection follows a documented process:

a. **Inbound interest.** Reciprocates discover HAND through the website, peer referrals, foundation introductions, or HAND's outreach.

b. **First conversation.** A 30-minute exploratory conversation with HAND staff. Both sides share what they're looking for. No commitments.

c. **Application.** A short written application: who you are, what you do, what you need, what you can give back, why HAND.

d. **Second conversation.** Deeper conversation, 60-90 minutes, exploring mutual fit. Includes another HAND staff member or Board member.

e. **Reference and verification.** HAND speaks to 2-3 references (Reciprocate's peers, prior collaborators, beneficiaries with the Reciprocate's permission).

f. **HAND internal review.** Staff and Reciprocate program lead review the application against the criteria in Section 2. Conflicts of interest disclosed.

g. **Decision.** Acceptance, declination, or "let's stay in touch" (for Reciprocates who are a strong fit but with timing or capacity constraints).

h. **Communication.** Decision communicated within 14 days of the second conversation, with reasoning.

i. **Onboarding.** Accepted Reciprocates sign an engagement agreement (Section 4) and begin the engagement.

The process typically takes 4-8 weeks from inbound interest to acceptance.

## Section 4. Engagement agreement

When a Reciprocate is accepted, a written Engagement Agreement documents:

a. **Names and parties.** HAND Protocol Foundation; the Reciprocate (and signatory if organizational).

b. **Term.** Default three-year initial term, renewable. Either party may withdraw on notice per Section 9.

c. **Scope of work.** What HAND will provide (web, brand, operational tools, Sovereign Reciprocates option, accompaniment cadence). Detailed in a separate Scope Annex updated annually.

d. **Cadence of contact.** Default: monthly working sessions, quarterly reflection, annual planning. Adjustable to the Reciprocate's actual pace.

e. **Mutual commitments.** What HAND commits to. What the Reciprocate commits to (responsiveness, engagement with Contributors, honest feedback).

f. **Confidentiality.** Information shared by the Reciprocate is held in confidence per Privacy Policy.

g. **Sovereignty.** Where Sovereign Reciprocates is part of the engagement, the Reciprocate Consent Framework ([`../policies/data-sovereignty-and-ai.md`](../policies/data-sovereignty-and-ai.md) §4) is attached.

h. **Recognition.** How HAND may speak publicly about the engagement (default: no public mention without case-by-case consent).

i. **Conflict resolution.** Disagreements addressed first directly, then through HAND's program lead, then through the Executive Director, then through Board mediation if necessary.

j. **Termination.** Either party may end the engagement on 60 days' written notice. Transitions handled per Section 9.

k. **Cost.** Default: free to the Reciprocate. HAND may negotiate cost-sharing in specific cases (e.g., for Reciprocates with budget capacity who want premium support); decisions are case-by-case and not the default.

Both parties sign and retain.

## Section 5. During engagement

a. **Program lead assigned.** Each Reciprocate has a designated program lead at HAND.

b. **Working rhythm.** Established in the first 30 days. Typically monthly sessions plus async communication.

c. **Quarterly reflection.** Joint review of what's working, what isn't, what to adjust.

d. **Annual planning.** What the next year looks like. Update Scope Annex.

e. **Contributor matches.** As Contributor needs arise, HAND-mediated matching per [`../policies/volunteer-and-contributor.md`](../policies/volunteer-and-contributor.md).

f. **Sovereign Reciprocates deployment.** Optional. If pursued, Reciprocate Consent Framework signed first; deployment follows the AI policy.

g. **Documentation.** HAND maintains an engagement file with session notes, deliverables, feedback, and Reciprocate communications. Reciprocate has access to their own file.

h. **Conflicts of interest.** Either party may raise. Resolution per the Engagement Agreement.

## Section 6. Reciprocate graduation

Graduation is the planned, healthy end of a Reciprocate engagement. It is **not** failure; it is the intended outcome.

### When graduation is appropriate

a. The Reciprocate has the infrastructure they need.

b. The Reciprocate can independently maintain the infrastructure (with optional alumni-tier support from HAND).

c. The relationship has been mutually rewarding and is reaching its natural arc.

d. The Reciprocate expresses readiness or HAND raises the conversation.

Graduation typically occurs at the end of Year 3 of the engagement, but may be earlier if mutual readiness exists.

### Graduation process

a. **Conversation.** Reciprocate and HAND staff discuss graduation 6 months in advance.

b. **Documented transition plan.** What does the Reciprocate need from HAND in the final 6 months to be fully independent? What stays with the Reciprocate? Who is HAND's alumni contact?

c. **Sovereignty handoff (if applicable).** Adapter weights, training data, evaluation logs, inference recipe handed over with documentation. Reciprocate signs receipt confirming the handoff. Sovereignty principle 2 operationalized.

d. **Final session.** A formal close: review of the engagement, lessons learned, Reciprocate's reflections, HAND's reflections.

e. **Alumni status.** The Reciprocate enters alumni status with HAND. Optional ongoing contact: quarterly check-ins for two years, then annual; access to HAND community spaces; option to return for renewed engagement if circumstances change.

f. **Documentation.** Engagement file finalized and archived per Document Retention Policy.

g. **Public acknowledgment.** With Reciprocate consent, HAND may publish a case study, mention the engagement in the Annual Report, or invite the Reciprocate to speak at a HAND convening.

### Alumni pathway

Graduation is entry into a broader pool, not exit from a program. Graduated Reciprocates remain part of HAND's resource pool with full optionality:

a. **Alumni Reciprocates remain part of HAND's community** indefinitely. Optional quarterly check-ins for two years post-graduation, then annual; access to community spaces; option to return for renewed engagement if circumstances change.

b. **Alumni may return as Contributors.** The same flywheel that supported them is now one they can feed. Returning Contributors apply per the standard Contributor process but enter with the relational trust of prior engagement; reference checks may be lighter where the Corporation already has direct working knowledge.

c. **Alumni may mentor incoming Reciprocates.** Cross-cohort connections are explicitly welcomed: a graduated Reciprocate can pair with an incoming Reciprocate in similar work to share what they learned, what surprised them, and what the engagement looked like up close. Mentorship pairings are voluntary on both sides and structured with light scaffolding by HAND staff.

d. **Alumni may serve as cross-cohort connectors** for HAND staff: helping with intake conversations, evaluation discussions, or program design where their lived experience is relevant. Stipends or honoraria may apply for substantial connector work.

e. **Alumni have unrestricted alumni-tier access** to publicly available HAND resources (open-source code, methodology, evaluation reports, the Annual Flywheel Health Check, the Annual AI Accountability Report).

f. **Sovereignty commitments do not end at graduation.** HAND continues to honor the eight Sovereign Reciprocates principles for the graduated Reciprocate's adapter for as long as it exists, including continued portability and revocation rights.

g. **No reciprocity quota.** Alumni participation is voluntary in every direction. A graduated Reciprocate who chooses no continuing engagement is fully welcomed; an alumni Contributor who later needs to Receive again is fully welcomed.

The alumni pathway is the resource pool's primary mechanism of self-reinforcement: each engagement, in time, contributes back to the pool that supported it.

## Section 7. Mid-engagement transition

Sometimes a Reciprocate is no longer the right fit before three years. Transitions handled with respect.

### When mid-engagement transition is appropriate

a. **Reciprocate's needs have evolved** beyond what HAND provides.

b. **HAND's capacity has shifted** and the engagement cannot be sustained.

c. **Reciprocate's circumstances have changed** (dissolution, merger, founder departure).

d. **Mutual fit has weakened** despite good-faith effort.

### Transition process

a. **Conversation.** Either party raises the question of transition.

b. **Joint deliberation.** Is this a temporary capacity issue resolvable by pausing? Or is this a real fit shift?

c. **Decision.** Continue, pause, or transition.

d. **Transition plan.** If transitioning, the next 60-90 days include completing in-flight work, handing off infrastructure, and sovereignty handoff.

e. **Alumni status.** Mid-engagement transitions still enter alumni status, with the same protections and offerings as graduation.

f. **Documentation.** Transition documented in the engagement file.

## Section 8. Reciprocate withdrawal

A Reciprocate may withdraw from the engagement at any time. HAND honors withdrawal without prejudice.

a. **Notice.** 60 days' written notice preferred; immediate withdrawal accepted if needed.

b. **Wind-down.** HAND completes in-flight commitments (a website near launch is finished; an active Contributor engagement is concluded).

c. **Sovereignty handoff.** Same as graduation if Sovereign Reciprocates has been deployed.

d. **No penalty.** No financial obligation, no contractual penalty. The relationship simply ends.

e. **Alumni option.** Withdrawing Reciprocates are welcome in alumni status.

## Section 9. HAND-initiated termination

In rare cases, HAND may terminate an engagement for cause.

### Grounds for HAND-initiated termination

a. **Conduct violating the Code of Ethics** (harassment, discrimination, financial impropriety, harm to staff or Contributors).

b. **Material misrepresentation** of the Reciprocate's work or status.

c. **Sustained unresponsiveness** despite repeated attempts.

d. **Mission incompatibility** discovered after engagement begins.

e. **Legal or safety risk** to HAND, staff, or other Reciprocates.

### Procedure

a. **Concern raised** through HAND's internal channels.

b. **Conversation with Reciprocate** documenting the concern and seeking response.

c. **Decision** by Executive Director (and Board Chair for material cases).

d. **Communication** to Reciprocate with written explanation.

e. **Transition or immediate close** per circumstances.

f. **Sovereignty handoff** is provided even in for-cause termination, **unless** withholding is required by court order or to prevent ongoing harm. HAND's sovereignty principles outlast disagreement.

g. **Documentation** retained per the Document Retention Policy.

## Section 10. Documentation and privacy

a. Engagement files are confidential.

b. Public communications about Reciprocates require explicit Reciprocate consent.

c. Aggregated, de-identified outcome data may be reported publicly; individual Reciprocate identification only with consent.

d. Reciprocate personal information protected per Privacy Policy.

## Section 11. Open questions

- **Cohort vs continuous selection.** Cohort 1 onboards roughly together; later years may shift to continuous selection. Operational complexity differs. Decide before Cohort 2.
- **Reciprocate graduation timing.** Three years is the default; some Reciprocates may benefit from longer or shorter. Build flexibility into the Engagement Agreement.
- **Cost-sharing for capacity Reciprocates.** When a Reciprocate has budget and could pay, should they? Drafted as case-by-case; consider whether to make this more explicit policy.
- **Alumni community structure.** Active design of the alumni community is a Year 2-3 project.
- **Reciprocate-to-Contributor pathway.** Some Reciprocate principals may want to become Contributors after graduation. Welcome; document explicitly.
- **Cross-Reciprocate mentorship.** Could be valuable; needs structure to prevent overburdening busy practitioners. Pilot in Year 2.

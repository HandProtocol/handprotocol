The Problem

Transformative work,
inadequate support.

HAND serves three communities of regenerative impact work: practitioners who heal, founders who build, and organizers who steward. The lines between them are porous; many Companions live in two of these worlds at once.

  1. 01

    Healers & Practitioners

    Hold space for community wellbeing but spend more time surviving than serving. Deep community trust, zero structural support.

  2. 02

    Impact Entrepreneurs

    Building regenerative-impact products and communities, but traditional startup infrastructure doesn't fit their values, and nonprofit infrastructure doesn't fit their model.

  3. 03

    Grassroots Organizations

    Doing vital community work (harm reduction, mutual aid, food access, land stewardship), often more established but still under-resourced for sustained outreach.

The categories are illustrative, not exhaustive. A herbalist running a community apothecary, a harm-reduction organizer whose work is also a healing practice, a regenerative-agriculture entrepreneur stewarding a co-op: if your work is in this spirit, it likely fits.

Common barriers they all face:

Operate informally or under-resourced
Lack consistent branding and fundraising tools
Offered short-term help that disappears after delivery
Spend more time surviving than serving

Why Healers First?

Healers sit at the intersection of community trust, cultural continuity, and collective wellbeing, yet they are often the least supported structurally.

They're also the connective tissue. Healers are practitioners, they build products and communities, and they partner with impact organizations.

Austin is our proving ground. What works here becomes a model for expansion.

Backstory

Where we're going,
what we believe.

HAND has been operating for over a year. Here's where the work is headed, what guides it, and who's behind it.

Long-term vision

Scale without losing locality.

HAND Protocol is designed to grow while keeping what matters most: local trust and real relationships.

  • Additional local impact sectors beyond healing
  • Replication in other cities with aligned communities
  • Deeper integration with regenerative funding and donation tools
  • Network effects where supported practitioners support each other

But none of that matters without proof. Austin is the proof.

Transparency & trust

We believe

  • Trust is built through consistency, not promises.
  • Infrastructure should serve people, not extract from them.
  • Impact must be verifiable, not performative.

All major milestones, budgets, and outcomes are documented and shared publicly.

The team

Building from within.

HAND Protocol is founded by practitioners and technologists rooted in Austin's wellness community, people who have experienced firsthand the gap between transformative healing work and the infrastructure needed to sustain it.

We're not building from the outside. We're building from within.

HAND Learn / Module 04 of 08

The skills that matter

⏱ About 40 minutes 🛠 The module people pin the most — that's fine

You know the motions. This module is technique — the small set of habits that separates "fighting with the AI" from "flowing with it." There are only five.

Skill 1 · Be specific about the outcome

Claude can't read your mind, but it's phenomenal at reading your words — so spend your effort on the words. Describe what the result should look like, not just that you want one.

Vague (coin flip)Specific (works)
"Make it better""Make the headline bigger and change the background to a soft cream"
"Add a contact thing""Add a contact section at the bottom with my email and Instagram, centered"
"It's broken, fix it""When I click the menu button on my phone, nothing opens. It should show the links."
"Make it professional""Calmer colors, more space between sections, fewer exclamation marks"

A useful trick: describe it like you're texting a friend who's good at this stuff. You'd naturally include where, what, and what it should end up like. That's the whole skill.

Skill 2 · One thing at a time

Big requests ("build my whole site with a gallery, a shop, a blog, and a newsletter") make big messes — for humans too. Small requests get built, checked, and confirmed in a tight loop:

  • Ask for one change.
  • Look at the result.
  • Then the next one.

Ten small rounds beat one giant round every single time — faster overall, and when something goes wrong you know exactly which step caused it.

Skill 3 · Keep sessions fresh

Everything in your conversation — every message, file change, and explanation — piles up in Claude's short-term memory (its context). A long, winding session makes Claude slower, more forgetful, and burns through your plan's usage faster.

The fix is one habit: new task, new session. Two ways to do it:

  • Type /clear in the prompt box — wipes the conversation and starts fresh in the same folder.
  • Or click + in the sidebar for a new session.

"But won't it forget what we were doing?" Yes — that's the point. Files stay on disk; only the chat memory resets. If you want continuity, end a session by asking Claude to write a note to its future self:

Copy into Claude Code — end of a work session 
Before we stop: create or update a file called NOTES.md summarizing what we did today, what's left to do, and anything a fresh session should know. Keep it under 20 lines.

Then a new session can start with "Read NOTES.md and let's continue." Related commands, for when you see them mentioned: /compact squeezes a long conversation down without fully clearing it, and /usage shows how much of your allowance the session is using.

Skill 4 · Plan mode for anything big

For a small tweak, just ask. For anything that feels big — "restructure my whole page," "add a photo gallery" — use plan mode: Claude researches and proposes a plan without touching your files. You read the plan in plain English, adjust it, and only then let it build.

  • How to enter it: click the mode selector (next to the send button) and choose Plan — or just type /plan followed by your request.
  • What you'll see: a written plan and a question — roughly "want me to go ahead?" You can say yes, or "change step 3 first," or "no, let's think differently."

Measure twice, cut once — except the measuring is free and takes ninety seconds.

Skill 5 · Know your permission modes

The mode selector next to the send button has a few settings. The ones that matter:

ModeWhat it meansWhen
ManualAsks before every change. You review each diff.Now, and whenever you're unsure
Accept editsFile edits happen without asking; commands still ask.Later, mid-flow, when reviewing every diff gets tedious
PlanRead-only: explores and proposes, changes nothing.Before anything big
One warning

You may also see a "bypass permissions" style option that skips all asking. Leave it alone — it's for experts running Claude in sealed-off environments. You lose your training wheels and your brakes.

When things feel weird

A short decision guide for the moments something's off:

  • Claude is doing something you didn't want → press the stop button. Then say what you actually wanted. No harm done.
  • The result looks wrong → describe the gap: "I expected X, I'm seeing Y." That sentence is 90% of debugging (Module 06 goes deeper).
  • Claude seems confused or is going in circles → /clear and re-ask with fresher, more specific words. A fresh session with a good prompt beats a long session with a tired one.
  • You're confused → ask Claude to explain, pin the section of this course that's fuzzy, or press I'm stuck. All three are winning moves.

Checkpoint

Technique check

1 · Which request will get the best result?

Specific outcome, specific place. Claude is only as good as the target you give it.

2 · You finished fixing the menu and now want to add a gallery. Best move?

New task, new session. Files stay; only the chat memory resets — keeping Claude sharp and your usage low.

3 · You want to add a whole new photo-gallery section — a big change. First move?

Plan mode explores without touching files. Review the approach before any building starts.

4 · Claude is mid-task doing something that looks wrong. You should…

The stop button is always allowed. Interrupt early, redirect clearly.

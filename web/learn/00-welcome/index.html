Welcome & how this works

⏱ About 15 minutes 🛠 Nothing to install yet

Who this course is for

This course is for people who are comfortable using a computer but have never written code — or tried once and bounced off. Maybe you run a small business, organize a community, make art, or just have an idea that won't leave you alone.

Until recently, turning an idea into a working app meant months of learning or thousands of dollars. That changed. Tools like Claude Code let you build by describing what you want in plain English — and this course teaches you to do that well.

You will not become a programmer in nine modules. You'll become something just as useful: a person who can direct one — an AI one that works fast, doesn't judge, and never gets tired of your questions.

What you'll actually build

By the end of this course you will have:

  • A working app or website you made — a personal page, a page for someone's business, or a small useful tool. Your choice, in Module 05.
  • A real link on the internet you can text to a friend.
  • The habits to keep building after the course ends — asking well, checking work, and recovering when things break.

Modules 00–04 teach you Claude Code itself, thoroughly — that's the foundation everything else stands on. Modules 05–08 are the build: plan, make, ship, level up.

Two ways to take it: guided or solo

Guided means a real person from HAND is walking through this with you — sitting beside you, on a call, or checking in over messages. The course keeps them in the loop: they can see which module you're on and where you've been stuck, so you never have to re-explain yourself.

Solo means you go at your own pace. Every lesson is written to stand on its own — nothing assumes a human is present. But you're still not alone: help is one button away (more on that below).

Good to know

You picked guided or solo when you created your account, but it's not a contract. Work solo at midnight, bring questions to your guide later — mix freely.

The three buttons that make this course different

1 · Your progress saves itself

When you're signed in, finishing a module's checkpoint saves automatically. Close the tab, come back next week on a different device — the course home shows exactly where you left off and a Continue button that takes you there.

2 · The pin — for "I sort of get it"

Next to every section heading there's a small pin button. When something makes partial sense — you followed it, but you couldn't explain it to a friend — pin it. You can add a note about what feels fuzzy. Pins collect on your review list, perfect for revisiting on your own or bringing to a guide.

Try it right now

See the pin button at the top-right of this section? Click it. Add a note if you like ("just testing"). Then visit your review list to see it saved — and come back here. That's the whole system. Use it shamelessly.

3 · "I'm stuck" — the human button

Bottom-right of every lesson there's an I'm stuck button. Pressing it does something most courses can't: it pings a real person at HAND, with your name, the lesson you're on, and whatever you write about what's happening. Someone will reach out — usually the same day.

There is no such thing as being embarrassingly stuck. The button exists because everyone — including the people who built this course — gets stuck. The only mistake is staying stuck quietly.

What you'll need

  • A computer — Mac (macOS 13 or newer) or Windows (Windows 10/11). Not a tablet or phone; you need a real keyboard and file system.
  • A Claude account with a paid plan — Claude Code is included in the Pro plan and up (the free plan doesn't include it). We'll set this up together in Module 02. If cost is a barrier, mention it to your HAND contact — that's a solvable problem, not a stop sign.
  • About 45–60 minutes per module, ideally with breaks between modules rather than in the middle of one.
Screens change, ideas don't

Claude Code updates often. If a button in this course is in a slightly different place on your screen, don't worry — the concepts hold. When in doubt, pin the section and keep moving.

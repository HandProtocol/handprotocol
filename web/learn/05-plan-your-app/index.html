HAND Learn / Module 05 of 08

Plan your app

⏱ About 60 minutes 🛠 By the end, version one of YOUR app exists

Practice is over. From here to the end of the course, you're building your thing — something you'd want to exist even if this course didn't. That caring is fuel; you'll debug longer and polish harder for a project that's real to you.

Pick your project

Three proven tracks — or bring your own idea. The one rule for this course: keep it a website-shaped project (pages people look at and click around — no accounts, no payments, no database yet). That keeps Module 07's "put it online" step one drag-and-drop.

Track A · A page for you

A personal site: who you are, what you make or do, how to reach you. The classic first project because you already know everything about the subject.

Track B · A page for someone you love

Your cousin's food truck, your friend's braiding studio, your neighborhood group. Menu or services, hours, location, photos, a "message us" link. Warning: this one tends to make someone cry (the good kind) in Module 07.

Track C · A tiny useful tool

A little machine that solves one small real problem: a tip splitter, a camping packing list, a "which chore is whose" wheel, a countdown to a date you care about. Slightly more ambitious — the app does something.

Choosing paralysis?

Take Track A. You can build B and C after the course — the skills transfer completely. Do not spend three days choosing; spend twenty minutes building and you'll know more about what you want than any amount of deliberating.

Give it a home

One project, one folder — remember? Make a fresh folder on your Desktop named after your project, lowercase-with-dashes: rosas-tacos, my-site, trip-splitter. In the Claude app, start a new session (the + in the sidebar) and Select folder → your new folder. Your practice folder retires with honor.

Write the brief — with Claude interviewing you

Professionals never build from a blank stare; they build from a brief — one page saying who it's for, what it must do, and how it should feel. Here's the trick: you don't have to write it. You answer questions; Claude writes it. Copy this in:

Copy into Claude Code 
I'm a beginner building my first real website with you, and I want to start with a proper brief. Interview me one question at a time (5–7 questions total) about: who it's for, what it needs to show or do, the 3–4 sections it should have, and the mood/style (I might name sites or brands whose look I like). Then write the finished brief to a file called BRIEF.md. Keep it under one page and in plain language.

Answer honestly and briefly — bullet-point answers are fine. When it's done, Claude will ask permission to create BRIEF.md. Read it before you accept: this is your project's north star, and it's the first document you ever spec'd. If a line is wrong, say so — "actually, the hours section matters more than the gallery" — and let it revise.

Scaffold version one — in plan mode

A whole site is a big change, and you know what we do with big changes: plan mode first. Copy this in:

Copy into Claude Code 
/plan Read BRIEF.md and propose how you'd build version 1 of this site. Requirements: plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript files only (no frameworks, no build tools), it must work by just opening index.html in a browser, it must look good on a phone, and use placeholder text/images anywhere I haven't given you real content. Tell me what files you'll create and what each section will contain.

Read the plan like a client, because you are one. Does it match your brief? Anything missing or extra? Push back freely — "skip the testimonials section, add a map instead." When the plan looks right, tell Claude to go ahead, and approve its changes as they come.

Why "no frameworks"?

Those requirements in the prompt keep your project simple: files you can open directly, host anywhere free, and understand with Claude's help. When you outgrow them — that's a great problem, for after graduation.

First look

When Claude finishes, open your project folder and double-click index.html. There it is — version one of your actual thing. Parts will be wrong. Placeholder photos will be goofy. The color might be off. Perfect. Wrong-but-existing is infinitely ahead of perfect-but-imaginary, and all of Module 06 is about closing the gap.

One more small ritual before the checkpoint — the session note habit from Module 04:

Copy into Claude Code 
Create NOTES.md: summarize what we built today, and list the top 5 things I said I wanted (from BRIEF.md) that aren't in version 1 yet. Under 20 lines.

Checkpoint

Version one exists

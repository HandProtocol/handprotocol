# Hack Club Bank · Fiscal Sponsorship

## TL;DR

HCB is a 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsor with a flat 7% revenue fee, no minimums, and the fastest published onboarding in the industry. The catch: HCB self-describes as **"primarily focused on teen-led projects, so we're more selective with adult-run organizations."** HAND is adult-led. Apply anyway — the intro call is free, and HCB's culture (open-source, transparent ledger, builder-friendly) is a real match for HAND's posture even if the demographic is off-pattern. Run Aspiration in parallel as the more mission-aligned backup.

## The program

> "One simple, transparent fee: 7% of revenue."
> "No legal fees. No startup fees. No transaction fees. No card issuing fees.
> No subscription fees. No check deposit fees. No credit card processing fees."

What HCB provides:
- 501(c)(3) umbrella — tax-deductible donations route through The Hack Foundation, HCB's parent
- Bank account, debit cards, check writing, ACH, wire
- Automatic 990 filing handled by The Hack Foundation
- HCB itself is open source — the platform you see is the platform you ledger on
- Environmental commitment: "funding will never come from the fossil fuel industry"

What HCB explicitly cares about:
- Teen-led projects are the default audience. Adult-led foundations face higher scrutiny.
- No exclusions disclosed on religion, politics, scope — selection happens in the intro conversation.

## Fit assessment

**Score: 3/5.** Honest split:

- **Culture fit: strong.** HCB is open-source-native, transparent-ledger-by-default, builder-led. HAND's posture (governance docs published openly, foundation finances on-chain via Sweetspot historically) maps to HCB's worldview better than to most foundation sponsors.
- **Demographic fit: weak.** Adult-led foundation seeking 501(c)(3) umbrella while a federal exemption is in progress. HCB will read this as "you should probably just finish filing." We need a real answer to "why HCB and not waiting on the IRS letter?"
- **Cost: excellent.** 7% flat, no other fees, fastest onboarding published. If accepted, this is the cheapest path to any 501(c)(3)-gated grant.

**The honest reason to apply**: the conversation. Even if HCB declines, the questions they ask in the intro call will sharpen the pitch for Aspiration and PPF. Cost of applying is one weekday's worth of attention.

### Anticipated follow-up: "Why HCB?"

HAND Protocol Foundation is filing as a 501(c)(3) public charity in Austin, Texas. Federal exemption is drafted but not yet submitted — realistic landing is Q4 2026 to Q2 2027. In the meantime, we have donors who'd like to give tax-deductibly, grant programs that gate on 501(c)(3) status, and an open-source AI workstream ("Sovereign Reciprocates") shipping production code now.

We picked HCB for the conversation, not just the umbrella. Three reasons:

1. **Transparency-by-default matches our posture.** HAND has been publishing governance documents, discovery research, and financial commitments openly since pivot. HCB's open ledger and open-source platform fit how we already work.
2. **Speed.** Most 501(c)(3) sponsors take months. HCB publishes "1-week intro call" as the standard. Mystic Hearts (our first Reciprocate-group product) is shipping in weeks, not quarters.
3. **Flat 7% with no other fees.** This is unusual. Most full-service sponsors stack monthly minimums, transaction fees, and bookkeeping costs that quietly hit 12–18% combined.

The umbrella covers the gap.

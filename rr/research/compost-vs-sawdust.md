# Compost-It vs. Just-Sawdust — Process & Maintenance Research (Reimagine Ranch)

**Status:** Research — 2026-05-29. Internal. Companion to [`compost-latrine.md`](./compost-latrine.md)
(which scopes three *hardware* forms in §1). **This doc is about the operating choice and the
labor:** do we **compost the material on site** (sawdust as the carbon input to a real composting
process) or **just cover with sawdust and haul it off** (collection-only)? And — the part that
actually decides it — **what does each cost in maintenance?**

> Bottom line up front: *Just-sawdust* is the simplest **toilet** but leaves you with a **disposal
> problem and no nutrient-loop story.** *Compost-it* is more land, patience, and protocol, but it's
> the only version that earns RR's headline adjectives (**nutrient-cycling, closed-loop, off-grid**)
> and ends with finished compost instead of a hauling bill. **Recommendation: design + permit for
> compost-it; let it be operated as just-sawdust on day one and graduate to composting as the
> steward routine matures.** Same as the build posture — *build once, upgrade the operation in place.*

---

## 1. The two operating modes, precisely

Both modes use **the same toilet** (a dry, waterless seat over a bucket or vault, with a bin of
cover material beside it). The fork is **what happens to the bucket's contents.**

### Mode A — "Just sawdust" (cover-and-collect)
- Sawdust is a **cover material only**: a scoop after every use to block odor, absorb moisture, and
  exclude flies. Nothing more is asked of it.
- Full buckets are **carried off and disposed** — bagged to trash (often illegal/again-problematic),
  taken to a transfer/septic/holding service, or emptied into a sealed holding drum for later
  hauling. **No on-site stabilization happens.** What goes in raw comes out raw.
- It is a **toilet**, not a sanitation system. Cheapest to stand up, **most expensive to operate**
  (you're paying — in money or trips — to move untreated waste forever).

### Mode B — "Compost it" (sawdust as the carbon input)
- Same sawdust cover at the seat — but now the sawdust is doing **double duty**: it's the
  **carbon half of a compost recipe**. Bucket contents (or a twin-vault's accumulation) are added
  to a **dedicated composting system** and processed to a **safe, finished, soil-like compost.**
- Two credible composting paths (mirrors `compost-latrine.md` §1a/1b):
  - **B1 — Thermophilic batch pile (Jenkins / "humanure" method):** deposits + cover go into a
    contained, insulated pile that **self-heats** as microbes work. A well-built pile exceeds
    **~160°F (71°C)**, which **kills all known human pathogens within ~24 hours**; the pile is built
    over a collecting period, then left **undisturbed to cure**.
  - **B2 — Moldering twin-vault:** two alternating chambers; one fills while the other rests
    ~12 months at ambient temperature. **Cooler and slower** (relies on time + worms/fungi, not
    high heat), so it needs **longer cure** and is **less certain on pathogen kill** than a hot pile —
    but it's lower-touch day to day and good where you can't tend a pile.

## 2. The science that makes "compost-it" safe

Cover material isn't decoration — it's the recipe and the odor engineering.

- **Carbon : Nitrogen ≈ 30:1** is the target for active composting. Human excreta (esp. urine) is
  **nitrogen-rich**; **sawdust is carbon-rich.** The cover scoop is what pulls the mix toward 30:1
  so it composts instead of putrefies. *Too little carbon → stink + slime; enough carbon → it
  composts.*
- **Sawdust as biofilter:** a generous cover layer **absorbs moisture** and acts as an **odor
  biofilter** — this is why a well-covered sawdust toilet genuinely doesn't smell.
- **Thermophilic kill (B1):** a properly built/maintained pile reaches **>160°F (71°C)** and
  destroys pathogens in **~24 h**. Lower temps still work given **time** — Jenkins' safe-reduction
  relationship is **T = 42 + 8/t** (T = °C, t = days), i.e. lower heat just needs longer.
- **Retention/cure (B1):** the standard humanure cycle is roughly **~1 year building** the pile +
  **~1 year curing undisturbed** → a **two-bin / two-year** rotation yields finished compost. (B2
  vaults run a similar ~12-month-per-side rest.)
- **Multi-barrier safety:** heat **and** time **and** cure — never rely on one. Finished humanure
  compost is typically directed to **non-edible / orchard / ornamental** use unless a full hot-cycle
  + long cure is documented; this is the conservative, grant-defensible posture.

## 3. Cover material — getting the sawdust right (applies to both modes)

| Factor | Guidance | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Coarseness** | **Coarse** shavings/sawdust beat **fine** table-saw dust | Fine dust waterlogs → sludge; coarse traps air pockets (aerobic) |
| **Moisture** | Slightly **moist**, not bone-dry, not sopping | Best odor block; kiln-dry fine dust under-performs + fills the bucket faster |
| **Source** | On-site/local **untreated** wood waste (no painted/PT/engineered) | Free byproduct, closes a local loop; no glues/preservatives in the compost |
| **Alternatives / blends** | Coir, dry leaves, peat-free duff, pine shavings | Coir holds more moisture (good for urine-heavy use); leaves are free; **avoid peat** (unsustainable) |
| **Recipe role** | It's the **carbon** toward 30:1, not just a lid | Under-covering is the #1 cause of odor + failed compost |

**RR sourcing note:** the ranch + local arborists already generate untreated wood waste, and the
**lumber plan** (`lumber-supply.md`) mills cedar — *clean offcut sawdust is a free byproduct of the
build itself.* Caveat: aromatic cedar/juniper dust is fine as cover but compost-purists prefer
neutral hardwood/pine shavings for the active pile; **keep a coarse, untreated, slightly-moist
shavings supply for the pile and use cedar dust mainly at the seat.**

## 4. Maintenance — the real decider

### 4.1 At the seat (identical in both modes)
- **Every use:** one scoop of cover material over deposits. *This is 90% of odor control.*
- **Keep the cover bin full** beside each stall; refill from a covered drum.
- **Wipe the seat / surfaces** as part of the gathering's normal restroom cleaning.
- **Urine:** **diverting urine** (or a soak-away) keeps solids drier, cuts odor, slows fill, and
  improves the C:N balance. Strongly recommended for the accessible stall at minimum.

### 4.2 Bucket / vault cadence
- **Bucket swap:** small bucket ≈ every **~5 days to 1–2 weeks** at light/steady use; large unit
  **2–4 weeks**. **At an event, plan per-day** — a gathering can fill a bucket in hours, so the
  steward stages **spare buckets + extra cover** and swaps live. Capacity, not composting, is the
  event constraint.
- **Twin-vault (B2):** no daily emptying — you **switch sides** when one fills (~annually) and
  empty the **rested** side.

### 4.3 The composting operation (Mode B only — the added labor)
| Task | Cadence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Add bucket contents to the pile, top with bulk cover | each emptying | always finish with a clean cover cap so the pile never smells/draws flies |
| Keep a **carbon "biofilter" cap** on the active pile | ongoing | a thick straw/shavings lid; deposits go *into* it, never exposed |
| **Monitor temperature** (compost thermometer) | weekly during active phase | confirm the hot phase (B1); log it — the log *is* the safety record |
| Maintain **moisture + bulk** | as needed | too dry → won't heat; too wet → goes anaerobic; add coarse bulk |
| **Do NOT turn** (Jenkins) or turn per your method | — | Jenkins piles are typically left unturned; vaults are never turned |
| **Cure undisturbed** | ~12 months after last addition | the patience step that makes it safe |
| **Harvest finished compost** | annually | rotate bins/vault sides; finished product to orchard/ornamental |
| **Records** | each cycle | dates, temp log, source, end-use — the grant + health-dept artifact |

### 4.4 Mode A's hidden maintenance (the disposal tail)
- **Recurring haul-off** of untreated waste: trips, fees, a holding drum, and the legal/῍ick risk of
  doing it wrong. **No end product, no story, indefinite cost.** This is why "simplest toilet" ≠
  "least maintenance over time."

## 5. Side-by-side

| Dimension | **A — Just sawdust** | **B — Compost it** |
|---|---|---|
| Day-one simplicity | ✅ highest | ⚠️ needs a pile/vault + protocol |
| Standing labor (at seat) | same (cover every use) | same |
| Ongoing **system** labor | ⚠️ recurring **haul-off** | ⚠️ pile tending + annual harvest |
| Lifetime cost | ❌ pays to move waste forever | ✅ inputs are free; output is compost |
| End product | ❌ none (waste) | ✅ finished compost (nutrient loop) |
| Odor (done right) | ✅ low (cover) | ✅ low (cover + biofilter cap) |
| Pathogen safety | ➖ punts it offsite | ✅ heat + time + cure (documented) |
| Land/space need | minimal | needs a screened compost yard (2-bin) |
| Failure mode | overflowing buckets at events | pile won't heat (too dry/wet/low-carbon) |
| Grant / mission story | ❌ weak | ✅ **the** story (closed-loop, off-grid) |
| Event throughput | bucket capacity | bucket capacity (compost the backlog after) |
| Steward skill | low | **moderate — needs a trained compost steward** |

## 6. Recommendation for RR (events + community land)

1. **Design + permit for compost-it (Mode B).** The structure already supports it
   (`compost-latrine.md`: vault-ready deck height, removable hatches, stack routing). It's the only
   mode that earns the mission adjectives and ends without a hauling bill.
2. **Operate Mode A on day one** if the steward routine isn't ready — cover-and-collect into a
   sealed holding drum — then **graduate to composting** as soon as a trained steward + a screened
   two-bin compost yard exist. *Don't let "we're not ready to compost yet" block opening the toilet.*
3. **Default system = Jenkins thermophilic batch (B1)** for the pilot: cheapest, highest pathogen
   certainty, best documentation story. Keep **twin-vault (B2)** as the option where nobody can tend
   a pile.
4. **Divert urine** (at least the accessible stall) — drier solids, less odor, slower fill, better
   C:N.
5. **Cover material:** stockpile **coarse, untreated, slightly-moist shavings** for the pile; use
   **cedar offcut sawdust from the build** at the seat. Keep both bins always full.
6. **Events are a capacity problem, not a compost problem:** staff spare buckets + cover for the
   gathering, then compost the accumulated material on the normal cycle afterward.
7. **Keep the records** (temp log, dates, end-use) from day one — the binder is the health-dept and
   grant artifact, and it's free if you start it early.

### Open decisions (for koH)
- **B1 (hot pile) vs B2 (moldering vault)** as the default? (Recommend **B1**.)
- **Urine diversion** yes/no, and where the urine goes (mulch basin / soak-away)?
- **Compost end-use policy:** orchard/ornamental only, or food-crop after a documented full cycle?
- **Who is the compost steward**, and is there a screened spot on the land for the two-bin yard?
- **Day-one mode:** open as A (collect) and graduate, or wait until B is fully stood up?

# Permaculture & Cheap Material Sourcing — Build Research (Reimagine Ranch)

**Status:** Research — 2026-05-30. Internal. Companion to [`lumber-supply.md`](./lumber-supply.md)
(takeoff, suppliers, retail pricing) and [`compost-latrine.md`](./compost-latrine.md)
(design, §5 materials, §7 locked dimensions). Where `lumber-supply.md` answers *"where do we buy
the wood,"* this doc answers the question one rung lower and more in the RR grain: **how do we get
most of the material for free, found, or near-free — and source it like a permaculture project,
not a lumberyard run.**

> RR posture: **build slowly, with as much community labor and local material as possible.** That
> sentence is the whole sourcing strategy. The cheapest board is the one that was already on the
> land or already in the waste stream; the most fundable board is the same one. Money is the *last*
> tier here, not the first.

---

## 0. The decision in one line

Source in **four tiers, cheapest first** — (1) **free & found** on-site and in Austin's reuse
streams, (2) **salvage/reuse** retail (ReStore, Materials Marketplace), (3) **local mill** of
fallen trees (Harvest Lumber / Texas Urban Sawmill, including milling RR's own cedar on the land),
and only then (4) **retail** from `lumber-supply.md` for the few things that must be bought new
(fasteners, metal roof, the touch-grade WRC). Spend money only where free can't safely reach.

---

## 1. The four-tier sourcing strategy

Work top-down: exhaust each tier for a given assembly before dropping to the next. Most of the
*structure* and *site work* can come from tiers 1–3; tier 4 shrinks to the irreducible bought items.

| Tier | What it is | Best for (latrine assemblies) | Cost | RR fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Free & found** | On-site cedar/juniper, arborist chips, urbanite, pallets, Buy-Nothing/Craigslist free piles | Footing pads, ramp gravel substitute, cover material, skirt/blocking, site paths, non-structural wood | **~$0** + hauling + labor | Highest — it's literally the mission |
| **2 — Salvage / reuse retail** | Habitat ReStore, Austin Materials Marketplace, Lifecycle/ReBuild-type centers | Siding, doors, hardware, windows, trim, dimensional lumber when stocked | **¼–½ retail** | High — keeps story, low cost |
| **3 — Local mill (fallen trees)** | Harvest Lumber, Texas Urban Sawmill, on-site portable milling | Siding, crown, character trim, fence-grade posts, decking accents | **$3–7/bf** or **~$55–200/hr** portable | High — the grant photo |
| **4 — Retail (new)** | `lumber-supply.md` Tier 3 chains + metal supplier | PT structure where salvage is unsafe, SS/HDG fasteners, metal roof, long clean WRC runs | full retail | Necessary minimum |

**Sequencing rule (ties to the build calendar in `lumber-supply.md` §5):** tier 1 and tier 3
have *lead time* (a ChipDrop load is 1–5 weeks out; green cedar needs drying; a portable mill needs
scheduling), so **start the free/found and milling channels in week 0 alongside the metal-roof
order.** Salvage (tier 2) is opportunistic — visit ReStore weekly from week 0 and grab what fits.
Retail (tier 4) is last and same-week.

## 2. Free & found materials (the tier-1 table)

Central-Texas / Austin channels, verified. "Caveats" is where the honesty lives — free material is
free because someone else didn't want the work or the liability, so each row carries a real catch.

| Material | Where to get it (Austin) | Caveats |
|---|---|---|
| **On-site Ashe juniper ("Texas cedar")** | Standing/fallen cedar already on RR's ~22 ac — cut, then mill (see §4) | Crooked, knotty; great for **posts, blocking, skirt, rough siding**, not long clean runs. Naturally oily + rot-resistant; usable **green, no kiln** — the one local wood that skips the drying wait. |
| **Arborist wood chips** | [ChipDrop](https://getchipdrop.com/) (free dump-truck load, 4–20 yd³, 1–5 wk wait) | You get the *whole* load, no size control, mixed species/quality. Use for **paths, mulch, erosion control, compost bulking**, and as a **carbon/cover-material backstop** for the latrine. Can't choose timing — request early. |
| **Sawdust / shavings (cover material)** | Local mills (Harvest Lumber, Texas Urban Sawmill), cabinet/woodshops, Buy-Nothing | The latrine's *consumable*. Must be **untreated, kiln/clean** sawdust — never from PT, painted, or MDF scrap. Confirm source species/cleanliness. |
| **Urbanite (broken concrete)** | Craigslist "free," demolition sites, [concrete reuse guides](https://www.concretenetwork.com/concrete/demolition/urbanite-recycled-concrete.html) | Free if you haul. Heavy, sharp, needs sorting. Permaculture-standard for **dry-stacked retaining, ramp/path base, footing pads, herb-spiral edging.** Not for finish surfaces; eye protection + gloves. |
| **Pallets** | [Craigslist free](https://austin.craigslist.org/search/zip?query=pallets), warehouse/retail backs, Buy-Nothing | **Only HT-stamped** (heat-treated). **Never MB** (methyl bromide — toxic). Unstamped = unknown, skip for anything people touch. Good for **blocking, jigs, temporary forms, non-touch skirt** — deconstruction labor is real. |
| **Free-pile dimensional lumber, doors, windows, hardware** | [Buy Nothing](https://buynothingproject.org/), [Austin Freecycle / trashnothing](https://trashnothing.com/austin-free), [Craigslist free](https://austin.craigslist.org/search/zip) | Inconsistent sizes, hidden rot/nails/fasteners, no warranty. Inspect for PT/lead paint on anything pre-1978. Best for **stall door, vent louvers, non-structural trim** — opportunistic, not plannable. |
| **Straw / clay / sand (natural-wall infill)** | Local hay/feed stores (straw bales), on-site soil, sand supplier | See §3 — relevant for a future cob/earthbag bench, screen wall, or thermal mass, not the raised latrine deck. Straw is cheap-not-free; clay/sand can be near-free on-site. |

**Free is not zero-cost.** Every tier-1 row trades **money for labor + hauling + inspection +
risk**. That trade is *exactly* the RR credit-loop's job — community labor turns a free pile into a
built thing, and that labor earns credits. The "$0 material" line only works because the labor line
is the community's contribution, not a vendor invoice.

## 3. The permaculture / natural-materials angle

Permaculture sourcing isn't just "cheap" — it's a posture: **use what the site and the local waste
stream already produce, cycle nutrients and materials in closed loops, and disturb the ground as
little as possible.** The latrine design already earns most of this (waterless, no-concrete piers,
nutrient-cycling). Sourcing extends it to the *materials themselves*:

- **On-site cedar = zero material-miles.** Ashe juniper is already on the parcel, is rot-resistant,
  and mills usable green. Cutting and milling it for posts/skirt/rough siding turns a land-management
  task (juniper is over-dense on most Central-TX parcels) into building stock — a closed loop.
- **Arborist chips close the carbon loop.** ChipDrop diverts a waste stream into RR's paths, compost
  bulking, and the latrine's cover-material backstop. "Local, free, natural by-product that doesn't
  require extra carbon to create" is literally how the provider describes it.
- **Urbanite is the permaculture darling for hardscape.** Dry-stacked broken concrete for path base,
  footing pads, and retaining keeps waste concrete out of the landfill *and* off the new-concrete
  carbon ledger — and it dovetails with the latrine's **"no concrete pour"** claim (we don't pour;
  we *reuse* set concrete cold, no cure, no new cement).
- **Earth / cob / straw for future near-free builds.** Cob's core inputs (clay, sand, straw) can run
  **$3k–6k for a small build** and are often partly site-sourced; one Texas builder reported a
  cordwood interior wall for **~$20 of purchased dirt + straw**. Not for the *raised* latrine deck
  (it wants dry framing off the soil), but ideal for a **later RR build** — a thermal-mass bench, a
  cob screen wall around the latrine patio, an earthbag tool-store, or a wash-station surround.
  Flagged here so the *pattern* is on record, not just the first build.

Every adjective this unlocks is **true of the actual plan**: site-sourced, waste-stream-diverted,
closed-loop, no-new-concrete, low-material-miles. Same discipline as the latrine doc — claim only
what the build earns.

## 4. Local milling — fallen trees & on-site cedar (tier 3)

This is the bridge between "free wood on the land" and "usable lumber," and it's where RR's
sourcing story is strongest. Two complementary channels, both verified:

| Source | Model | RR use |
|---|---|---|
| **[Harvest Lumber Co.](https://www.harvestlumberco.com/)** (E. Austin urban sawmill) | Mills fallen/felled Central-TX trees with **documented log provenance**; buy rough-sawn by the board foot at the yard | The **grant-photo** source for siding/crown/character trim — "a board with a story." Already RR's tier-1 mill in `lumber-supply.md`. |
| **[Texas Urban Sawmill](https://www.txurbansawmill.com/)** (Dripping Springs; Benefit Company) | "Community sawmill of Central Texas" — tree rescue + salvage milling; **portable/onsite milling available**, logs otherwise hauled to their facility | Can mill **RR's own felled cedar/juniper on the land** (or nearby), turning land-clearing into building stock. Also a [USDA Wood Innovations Grant](https://www.txurbansawmill.com/articles/woodinnovationsgrant) participant — a real grant thread (see §6). |
| **On-site portable milling** | Portable bandmills run roughly **$55–$200/hr** depending on log size/service; some add ~$7/mi transport | Mill RR's downed cedar **where it falls** — minimal hauling, maximal provenance. Ashe juniper mills green (no kiln), so it goes straight from log to post/skirt/rough siding. Crooked/knotty form limits it to short or character pieces — pair with bought WRC for long clean runs (decking, rails), exactly the split in `lumber-supply.md` §1. |

**The move:** clear the cedar RR needs to clear anyway → mill it on-site or at Harvest → use it for
every non-structural, non-long-run wood part of the latrine (posts, skirt, blocking, rough siding,
fence-grade pieces). Buy WRC only for the decking/rails that need length and straightness.

## 5. Tools without buying tools

Free/salvage material is moot without tools, and RR shouldn't buy a tool it uses once.

- **[Austin Public Library Tool Library](https://library.austintexas.gov/digital/do-it-yourself)**
  at **Recycled Reads, 5335 Burnet Rd** — drills and building tools check out for **two weeks, no
  late fees, renewable 5×** on a free Austin library card ($22 eCard for non-residents). The
  **seed library** (Central branch) is a bonus for any planting around the build.
- **[East Austin Tool Lending Library](https://www.facebook.com/austintoollendinglibrary/)** —
  community tool lending; verify current hours/status before relying on it.
- **Community-owned tools via the credit loop** — tools the build genuinely needs repeatedly
  (a pier driver, a circular saw) are better held by the community and lent against credits than
  bought per-volunteer. Borrow first, buy shared only on repeat need.

## 7. Projected savings vs. the `lumber-supply.md` budget

`lumber-supply.md` §4 sizes **Path A (community weekend, 3×3) at ~$2,500–3,500 materials.** That
budget already assumes some salvage cedar. Layering the four-tier strategy on top displaces bought
material with free/found/milled stock. Conservative line estimates:

| Assembly | Path-A retail (approx) | Tier-1–3 substitution | Substituted cost | Saved |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Footings / ramp base / paths | gravel + pier blocks ~$350 | **urbanite** path/base + **chips** paths; keep bought piers | ~$150 | **~$200** |
| Skirt, blocking, non-touch framing | ~$300 | **on-site milled cedar** + HT pallets | ~$50 (mill time/labor) | **~$250** |
| Rough siding / crown character wood | ~$500 (mill cedar) | **on-site milled** RR cedar vs. bought-bf | ~$200 (mill time) | **~$300** |
| Stall door / vent louvers / trim | ~$250 | **ReStore / Buy-Nothing** salvage | ~$60 | **~$190** |
| Cover material (consumable, ongoing) | small recurring buy | **free mill sawdust + ChipDrop** backstop | ~$0 | **~$50–100/yr** |
| Tool rental/buy | ~$150 assumed | **APL Tool Library** + shared tools | ~$0 | **~$150** |
| **One-time subtotal** | | | | **~$1,100–1,200** |

**Headline:** the four-tier strategy can pull a Path-A pilot from **~$2,500–3,500 down to roughly
$1,400–2,400 in cash** — on the order of **35–45% off the materials budget** — with the gap
converted into **community labor + hauling** (i.e., into credit-loop activity, which is a feature,
not a cost). The irreducible cash floor is the genuinely-must-buy set: **metal roof, SS/HDG
fasteners, long clean WRC decking/rails, precast piers, finish oil.** Free can't safely reach those.

> Caveat, honestly: these are *substitution* estimates, not quotes, and free material front-loads
> **labor and time** (deconstruction, hauling, milling, inspection, drying). The savings are real but
> they're paid in volunteer hours — which is precisely what RR has and what the credit-loop is for.

## 8. How this strengthens the grant story

`lumber-supply.md` §6 lists the latrine's true sustainability adjectives. Permaculture sourcing
*deepens each one with evidence a funder can see and a site tour can touch:*

- **Locally sourced → site-sourced.** Not just "Austin trees" — *RR's own cedar*, cleared as land
  management and milled on the parcel. Lower material-miles than even Harvest Lumber.
- **No concrete → reused concrete.** Urbanite turns the "no-pour" claim into an active
  waste-diversion claim: we keep set concrete out of the landfill instead of buying new cement.
- **Waste-stream diversion, quantified.** ChipDrop loads, salvaged ReStore lots, and milled fallen
  trees are all *measurable* diversions — the kind of number a circular-economy or Materials-
  Marketplace-adjacent funder rewards.
- **In-kind match built in.** LCRA-style grants count donated material + volunteer labor as match;
  RR's credit-loop *is* that match, documented in the ledger. The sourcing plan and the funding
  plan are the same plan.
- **A replicable, fundable method.** This isn't latrine-specific — it's RR's sourcing doctrine for
  every future build (bench, shade, wash station, cob screen). "Here's how we build cheaply and
  regeneratively, again and again" is a stronger grant narrative than one clever toilet.

Every claim above is true of the actual plan. That's the rule, same as the other RR docs.

## 9. Open decisions (feed the procurement plan)

1. **On-site milling:** mill RR's own cedar (portable on-site vs. haul to Harvest), or buy all cedar
   by the board foot? *Recommendation: mill on-site for posts/skirt/rough siding, buy WRC for runs.*
2. **Urbanite sourcing:** actively haul free concrete for footing pads/path base, or keep bought
   precast piers for the structural footings? *Recommendation: urbanite for paths/base + non-load
   pads; keep engineered piers under the deck.*
3. **ChipDrop timing:** request a load in week 0 (1–5 wk lead) for paths + cover backstop?
   *Recommendation: yes — it's free and front-loaded.*
4. **Pallet policy:** allow HT-stamped pallets for non-touch blocking/forms, or skip entirely?
   *Recommendation: HT-only, non-touch uses only; never MB/unstamped.*
5. **Natural-wall pilot:** is a cob/earthbag element (bench, screen wall, tool-store) in scope for a
   *later* RR build? *Flag for the roadmap; out of scope for the raised latrine itself.*
6. **501(c)(3)-gated grants:** sequence the Lifecycle-style material grants behind HAND's filing —
   confirm timeline with the fiscal workstream.
